TASK. Place this company inside the population distributions of its revenue runway: where it belongs for next year's growth and for how long that growth persists, and why. The distributions are in section R; they describe companies in a similar state to this one (section S). Use the filed figures (F), the latest quarters (Q, when shown), the company's own documents (D) and the company's state (S).

{{INPUTS}}

HOW TO ANSWER
- "percentile": a number from 5 to 95. 50 is the typical company in a similar state; 80 means you expect this company to be above 80 percent of them. Do not default to 50: put the company where its evidence puts it, and no further.
- A percentile below 10 or above 90 needs hard evidence: at least one citation from section D or Q whose quote holds a firm commitment (a contract, an agreement, backlog, an award, a purchase order) or a figure together with company guidance, capacity or a stated plan. A general outlook sentence is not hard evidence.
- "width": narrow, normal or wide. How sure you are of the position. Narrow means the evidence pins the value down; wide means it could plausibly be far from your position either way.
- "reasons": one to six short statements of why the company belongs there.
- "citations": one to six objects {"section": "F" or "Q" or "D" or "S", "quote": text}. Each quote is 12 to 600 characters of contiguous text copied exactly from that section (no ellipsis, no paraphrase, no changes to numbers or punctuation).
- "bull_case" and "bear_case": one sentence each, with a citation, stating the strongest evidence-based case for a higher and for a lower position on revenue growth.
