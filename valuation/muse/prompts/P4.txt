HOW TO ANSWER
- "percentile": a number from 5 to 95. 50 is the typical company in a similar state; 80 means you expect this company to be above 80 percent of them. Do not default to 50: put the company where its evidence puts it, and no further.
- A percentile below 10 or above 90 needs hard evidence: at least one citation from section D or Q whose quote holds a firm commitment (a contract, an agreement, backlog, an award, a purchase order) or a figure together with company guidance, capacity or a stated plan. A general outlook sentence is not hard evidence.
- "width": narrow, normal or wide. How sure you are of the position.
- "reasons": one to six short statements of why the company belongs there (durability of its advantages, size of its market, the pressure on its position).
- "citations": one to six objects {"section": "F" or "D" or "S", "quote": text}. Each quote is 12 to 600 characters of contiguous text copied exactly from that section (no ellipsis, no paraphrase, no changes to numbers or punctuation).
- "risks": exactly three objects {"risk": one sentence, "input": the name of the input it would move most (one of {{ALL_INPUT_NAMES}}), "citation": {"section": ..., "quote": ...}}, the three most material risks to the company's outlook, each backed by a quote from the text.
