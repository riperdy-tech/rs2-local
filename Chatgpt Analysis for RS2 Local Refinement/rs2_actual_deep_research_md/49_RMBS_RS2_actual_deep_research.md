## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Rambus is tied to semiconductor cycles, AI data-center capex, memory standards, and customer inventory behavior. AI inference and agentic workloads are increasing demand for memory bandwidth and secure, efficient data movement [Actual from company release]. That is a powerful secular tailwind.

The macro risk is that semiconductor capital spending and customer ordering can be cyclical. If AI infrastructure spend pauses or memory customers digest inventory, near-term product revenue can disappoint even if the long-term standard remains healthy.

Rates matter through multiple compression. High-P/E semiconductor names can fall sharply if growth expectations reset.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Semiconductor IP companies can be high-quality businesses because licensing and royalties can scale with attractive margins. The base rate improves when a company is embedded in important standards. However, semiconductor names with AI narratives often get priced for perfection before revenue fully catches up.

Rambus's Q1 2026 revenue growth of 8.1% is solid but not explosive relative to a 50x+ trailing P/E [Actual]. Product revenue growth of 15% is more encouraging [Actual]. The base-rate question is whether product growth accelerates enough to validate the valuation.

Base-rate score: positive business, valuation-sensitive stock.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

AI workloads create second-order demand for memory bandwidth, which can benefit interface chips, IP royalties, and adjacent security/connectivity products. If standards adoption expands, Rambus can enjoy durable revenue without owning the entire chip system.

The negative second-order effect is customer concentration and purchasing timing. A supply-chain disruption or customer pause can have outsized effects on product revenue, as investors saw when prior guidance softness hit the stock earlier in 2026 [Actual from news coverage].
