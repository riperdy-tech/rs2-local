## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Howmet Aerospace is a premier aerospace supplier with leading positions in jet engine components, fastening systems, engineered structures, and forged wheels. Its highest-quality exposure is commercial aerospace engines and spares, where demand is supported by aircraft production, engine utilization, and aftermarket replacement.

Q1 2026 was excellent. Revenue was $2.31B [Actual], up 19% year over year. Adjusted EPS was $1.22 [Actual], up 42%. Growth was driven by commercial aerospace up 20%, defense aerospace up 10%, and gas turbines up 39% [Actual]. Engine Products revenue was up 29% year over year, and Engine Products adjusted EBITDA margin reached 36.6% [Actual].

FY2025 also showed strong execution. Full-year 2025 revenue was approximately $8.3B [Actual], up 11%. Free cash flow was $1.43B [Actual], with 93% conversion of adjusted net income after record capex of $453M [Actual]. The company deployed about $1.2B of cash in 2025 to buybacks, preferred redemption, debt paydown, and dividends [Actual].

The issue is valuation. HWM closed at $270.41 [Actual], with market cap around $101B-$108B depending on source/share count [Actual]. At a quoted P/E above 60x [Actual/Estimate] and a dividend yield below 0.2% [Actual/Estimate], the stock is priced for continued exceptional growth and margin expansion. Even on annualized Q1 adjusted EPS of $4.88 [Estimate], the multiple is about 55x [Estimate].

RS2 conclusion: HWM is a Hold. The business quality is excellent, but the stock price has outrun a conservative margin-of-safety framework. Buy zone begins closer to $215-$225 unless guidance and EPS power move materially higher.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Commercial aerospace demand remains favorable. Aircraft production backlogs, airline fleet modernization, engine utilization, and spares demand support suppliers such as Howmet. Engine components and fastening systems benefit from both original equipment and aftermarket demand.

Defense aerospace is supportive. Geopolitical tensions, defense budgets, and aerospace platform demand support stable demand. HWM's defense aerospace market grew 10% in Q1 2026 [Actual].

Gas turbines are a newer tailwind. Q1 gas turbines revenue grew 39% [Actual], likely supported by power generation demand, data-center/electrification infrastructure, and industrial turbine activity.

Supply chain and input costs remain the macro risks. Aerospace supply chains are capacity-constrained, and Howmet depends on titanium, nickel, aluminum, energy, labor, and highly specialized manufacturing. Tariffs and trade restrictions can pressure costs or customer schedules.

Macro score: very favorable demand, but capacity/input constraints and valuation sensitivity are high.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Base rate for aerospace suppliers: high-quality suppliers can compound well during production and aftermarket upcycles, especially when they have proprietary processes and qualified parts. However, aerospace cycles are long and can reverse.

Base rate for engine-component suppliers: engine parts can earn high margins because qualification requirements are stringent and switching costs are high. This supports premium valuation.

Base rate for premium industrials at very high multiples: even great companies can underperform when expectations are too high. Multiple compression can dominate operational success.

Base rate for capacity expansions: capex backed by customer commitments can create growth, but execution, hiring, yield, and throughput must be proven.

Base-rate conclusion: HWM is a top-tier industrial, but current valuation creates a poor base rate for new-money returns unless growth keeps surprising.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Howmet's Engine Products segment is the crown jewel. Q1 2026 Engine Products revenue increased 29%, and adjusted EBITDA margin reached 36.6% [Actual]. This is an extraordinary industrial margin and reflects pricing, mix, spares, and manufacturing leverage.

The company benefits from structural aerospace qualification barriers. Parts used in engines and aircraft require certification, material expertise, precision manufacturing, and customer trust. This creates durable competitive advantage.

The business is diversified across commercial aerospace, defense aerospace, gas turbines, and other markets. Commercial aerospace is the dominant driver, but defense and gas turbines add resilience.

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly. In 2025, Howmet repurchased about $700M of common stock at an average price near $161, retired preferred stock, reduced debt, and paid dividends [Actual].

Business quality caveat: margin expectations are very high. When a segment already earns mid-30s adjusted EBITDA margins, future upside depends on mix, pricing, productivity, and capacity utilization. Any slowdown can compress expectations.

Business quality score: 14/15. HWM is an exceptional aerospace supplier; deduction is mainly for cycle/capacity risk.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Engine spares growth has second-order margin impact. Spares usually carry better margins than original equipment, so flight-hour recovery and engine utilization can expand earnings faster than revenue.

Capacity expansion has second-order customer-lock-in. If Howmet expands capacity under customer commitments, it can deepen long-term customer relationships and secure future revenue.

High valuation has second-order fragility. If the company beats numbers but the beat is smaller than investor expectations, the stock can fall despite good fundamentals.

Input inflation has second-order mix effects. If titanium, energy, or labor costs rise, pricing pass-through and productivity become central to margin defense.
