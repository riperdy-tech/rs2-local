## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

AbbVie is a high-quality global biopharma company that has largely answered the first phase of the post-Humira transition. Skyrizi and Rinvoq are now large enough to offset Humira erosion, neuroscience is growing, and management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance after Q1. The remaining question is valuation discipline.

Q1 2026 worldwide net revenue was $15.002B [Actual], up 12.4% reported and 10.3% operational [Actual]. Immunology revenue was $7.290B [Actual], up 16.4% reported, driven by Skyrizi at $4.483B [Actual], up 30.9%, and Rinvoq at $2.119B [Actual], up 23.3%. Humira fell to $688M [Actual], down 38.6%, but the company is now demonstrating that Humira erosion is no longer the dominant growth story.

Neuroscience is the second major pillar. Q1 2026 neuroscience revenue was $2.875B [Actual], up 26.0% reported. Vraylar generated $905M [Actual], Botox Therapeutic $1.009B [Actual], Ubrelvy $339M [Actual], Qulipta $296M [Actual], and Vyalev $201M [Actual]. Oncology was flat-to-down, and aesthetics improved but remains cyclical.

The valuation is the restraint. ABBV closed around $261 [Actual], with market cap around $461B [Actual]. Against raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $14.08-$14.28 [Actual], the stock trades near 18.4x midpoint adjusted EPS [Estimate]. The dividend yield is about 2.6%-2.7% [Actual/Estimate]. That is fair for a high-quality pharma compounder, but not deeply discounted after the stock hit a new 52-week high.

RS2 conclusion: ABBV is a Hold / buy-on-pullback. The business quality and post-Humira execution are strong enough to own, but the stock no longer offers a large margin of safety at current levels. A better entry would be below $235-$240 or after Q2/Q3 data confirm that Skyrizi/Rinvoq growth remains durable under competition and IRA/payer pressure.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

AbbVie's macro environment is mixed-positive. Demand for immunology, neuroscience, oncology, aesthetics, and migraine therapies is structurally supported by chronic disease prevalence, aging, and treatment innovation. However, biopharma faces constant pricing, reimbursement, regulation, and patent-cycle pressure.

The most important company-specific macro shift is that Humira biosimilar erosion is now largely a known variable. Humira sales declined 40.3% operationally in Q1 2026 [Actual], but Skyrizi and Rinvoq are carrying immunology growth. This lowers the uncertainty that dominated AbbVie's 2023-2025 story.

Pricing policy remains a significant risk. The U.S. Inflation Reduction Act, Medicare negotiation, payer utilization management, and global reimbursement pressure all matter. Botox and major immunology products can attract payer scrutiny, and high biologic prices remain politically exposed.

Aesthetics is more macro-sensitive. Botox Cosmetic grew 20.2% reported in Q1 2026 [Actual], but Juvederm declined 2.9% operationally [Actual]. Consumer discretionary weakness can pressure aesthetics even while therapeutic products remain resilient.

Macro score: favorable for therapeutic demand, mixed for pricing and aesthetics, and less uncertain than the peak Humira-cliff period.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Base rate for post-patent-cliff pharma: the best companies can replace lost blockbusters through next-generation drugs and acquisitions, but the market usually demands proof. AbbVie has provided more proof than most through Skyrizi and Rinvoq.

Base rate for immunology franchises: high-volume inflammatory-disease markets are attractive but fiercely competitive. AbbVie competes against J&J, Lilly, Amgen, UCB, Novartis, BMS, and others across psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's, ulcerative colitis, rheumatoid arthritis, atopic dermatitis, and related indications.

Base rate for large-cap pharma M&A: acquisitions can refresh growth but add debt, amortization, contingent liabilities, and integration risk. AbbVie has a strong record from Allergan in some areas, but debt remains high.

Base rate for dividend pharma: dividend growth can support total return, but payout quality depends on future patent durability, pipeline success, and cash flow after debt service.

Base-rate conclusion: AbbVie has transitioned from "cliff uncertainty" to "quality compounder." The valuation should be higher than during the Humira panic, but entry price still matters.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

AbbVie's core strength is therapeutic depth. It has major franchises in immunology, neuroscience, aesthetics, oncology, and eye care/other specialty areas. The company combines high-margin global products, lifecycle management, clinical development, and shareholder returns.

Immunology quality is high. Skyrizi and Rinvoq generated combined Q1 2026 revenue of $6.602B [Actual], more than 9.5x Humira's $688M [Estimate]. Skyrizi's Q1 growth of 30.9% [Actual] and Rinvoq's 23.3% growth [Actual] show the replacement engine is working. The key risk is competitive intensity and eventual patent/payer pressure.

Neuroscience quality is improving. Vraylar, Botox Therapeutic, Ubrelvy, Qulipta, and Vyalev provide a diversified growth set. Qulipta and Ubrelvy growth above 40%-50% [Actual] reflects migraine-market traction, while Botox Therapeutic remains a durable brand.

Oncology is the weaker area. Q1 oncology revenue declined 0.2% reported and 3.0% operational [Actual]. Imbruvica declined 24.7% [Actual], offset partly by Venclexta and Elahere. Oncology still has pipeline optionality, but current portfolio momentum is mixed.

Aesthetics remains valuable but cyclical. Botox Cosmetic grew 20.2% reported [Actual], while Juvederm was essentially flat reported and down operationally [Actual]. This segment deserves a lower multiple than core therapeutics because discretionary demand can fluctuate.

Business quality score: 13/15. AbbVie has proven commercial execution, diversified growth engines, and strong margins. Deductions: debt, immunology competition, oncology weakness, and non-GAAP dependence.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

The post-Humira transition has a second-order multiple effect. Once investors believe Skyrizi/Rinvoq replacement is proven, AbbVie can trade closer to a durable-growth pharma multiple rather than a cliff-risk multiple. Much of that rerating has now occurred.

Skyrizi and Rinvoq concentration is the next second-order risk. The market has moved from "Humira dependency" to "Skyrizi/Rinvoq dependency." If competitors pressure these products, the new thesis weakens.

Debt issuance has a second-order capital-allocation effect. AbbVie issued $8.0B of unsecured senior notes in Q1 2026 [Actual], raising cash but increasing gross debt. This supports flexibility but keeps interest expense and rating discipline important.

IPR&D charges have a second-order interpretation issue. They depress adjusted EPS in guidance when included, but recurring business-development spending may be part of the true cost of maintaining AbbVie's growth engine.

Pipeline manufacturing investments have long-term value. The $1.4B Durham campus and $380M North Chicago API investment [Actual] can improve resilience and support future products, but also reflect ongoing capex needs.

## SECTION 4. Scenarios

Bull case:

Skyrizi and Rinvoq sustain 20%+ combined growth, neuroscience remains above 15%, aesthetics normalizes, oncology stabilizes, and pipeline assets create new growth. Adjusted EPS compounds high single digits from the $14.18 midpoint. At 20x EPS, fair value can reach $300-$325 [Assumption].

Base case:

Skyrizi/Rinvoq continue growing but decelerate, Humira erosion becomes less relevant, neuroscience remains solid, aesthetics grows low-to-mid single digits, and oncology stays mixed. Adjusted EPS grows mid-single digits. Fair value: $235-$270 [Assumption].

Bear case:

Competition pressures Skyrizi/Rinvoq, neuroscience slows, Imbruvica declines faster, aesthetics weakens, and future IPR&D spending remains high. The stock rerates to 14x-15x adjusted EPS. Fair value: $190-$215 [Assumption].

Stress case:

Major safety/regulatory issue or payer action hits Skyrizi/Rinvoq while leverage remains high. Pipeline setbacks and policy pressure create a broad pharma derating. Downside could be below $180 [Assumption].

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

AbbVie's strongest horizon edge was in the years when investors doubted post-Humira replacement. That edge is now smaller because the market has seen Q1 2026 proof and the stock has reached a new 52-week high.

There is still a multi-year edge if Skyrizi/Rinvoq, neuroscience, and pipeline assets sustain growth longer than the market expects. But the near-term asymmetry is no longer obvious.

Best horizon setup: buy on a pullback caused by temporary concern over competition, acquired IPR&D charges, or aesthetics softness, provided immunology and neuroscience fundamentals remain intact.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

Bullish trap: assuming the post-Humira transition is completely solved. It is much improved, but Skyrizi/Rinvoq now carry large expectations.

Bearish trap: anchoring to Humira decline and ignoring that Humira is now much smaller than Skyrizi plus Rinvoq.

Accounting trap: treating adjusted EPS as pure recurring earnings without considering recurring business-development and IPR&D spending.

Momentum trap: buying because ABBV hit a fresh high after strong results. Quality can still be fairly valued.

Behavioral rule: buy ABBV when investors worry about temporary noise, not when they are fully rewarding transition success.
