## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Kiniksa is a profitable specialty biopharma company built around ARCALYST in recurrent pericarditis. Q1 2026 ARCALYST net product revenue was $214.3 million, up 56% year over year [Actual]. Management raised 2026 ARCALYST net sales guidance to $930 million to $945 million from $900 million to $920 million [Actual]. Q1 2026 net income was $22.6 million, diluted EPS was $0.27, and cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments rose to $468.1 million [Actual].

The growth is excellent, but the valuation already reflects it. At $64.53, KNSA has a $5.32 billion market cap, quoted P/E of about 70.9x, and EPS of $0.91 [Actual]. The market is capitalizing a long runway for ARCALYST plus pipeline optionality from KPL-387.

Core view: Hold. KNSA is a strong execution story, but after the run-up the stock requires continued ARCALYST growth and pipeline progress.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

KNSA is less tied to GDP and more tied to specialty-drug adoption, reimbursement, payer access, and clinical practice patterns. Recurrent pericarditis is a niche market, but strong therapy duration and prescriber expansion can create durable revenue.

Drug-pricing policy is a macro risk. Specialty biologics with high annual costs can face payer scrutiny, step edits, or policy pressure. Rates affect valuation because much of KNSA's value is in future growth.

Macro conclusion: medically driven demand, valuation and reimbursement sensitivity.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Single-product specialty pharma companies can compound rapidly when the product is clinically differentiated and underpenetrated. The base-rate warning is concentration: once growth slows, multiples can compress quickly unless the pipeline broadens.

KNSA's base rate is better than many single-product stories because ARCALYST has five years of launch experience, expanding adoption, more than 4,550 recurrent-pericarditis prescribers since launch, and average therapy duration approaching three years [Actual].

Base-rate score: positive business, concentration-limited stock.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

ARCALYST has attractive economics and strong growth in a focused indication. Kiniksa's commercial execution appears strong, with both new and repeat prescribers contributing to momentum [Actual]. Cash is increasing while the company funds pipeline development, which is a healthy signal.

The weakness is dependence on one franchise. KPL-387 Phase 2 recurrent pericarditis data are expected in 2H 2026, and a Phase 3 pivotal trial is expected by year-end [Actual]. That pipeline progress matters because ARCALYST concentration is high.

Quality score: 7/10. Strong growth and profitability, but narrow product base.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Therapy duration is the key second-order effect. If average ARCALYST duration continues to grow, patient starts become more valuable and revenue becomes more durable. This improves lifetime value without needing equivalent new-patient growth.

Pipeline optionality is the second effect. KPL-387 success could extend Kiniksa's pericarditis franchise and reduce single-product risk. Failure would reinforce concentration concerns.

## SECTION 4. Scenarios

Bear case: ARCALYST growth slows, payer friction rises, and pipeline data disappoint. Fair value falls toward the $40s [Estimate].

Base case: ARCALYST meets raised guidance, duration remains strong, and KPL-387 progresses. Fair value $60 to $68 [Estimate].

Bull case: ARCALYST exceeds guidance and KPL-387 data are strong. Fair value $80+ [Estimate].

Probability-weighted view: balanced after strong stock performance.
