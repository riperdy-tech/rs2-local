## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Cirrus Logic is a high-margin semiconductor company best known for audio and mixed-signal components, with a large customer concentration in smartphones. Fiscal Q4 2026 revenue was $448.5 million, GAAP EPS was $1.56, and non-GAAP EPS was $1.95 [Actual]. Full fiscal year 2026 revenue was a record $2.0 billion, up 5%, with GAAP EPS of $7.85, non-GAAP EPS of $9.26, free cash flow of $635.8 million, and about $1.2 billion of cash and investments with no debt after $280 million of share repurchases [Actual].

At $146.24, CRUS trades at about 15.8x FY2026 non-GAAP EPS and 19.3x trailing GAAP EPS [Actual/Estimate]. Adjusting for cash, the valuation is lower. The balance sheet is excellent. The main issue is customer concentration and smartphone cyclicality.

Core view: attractive on weakness. CRUS is financially strong and reasonably valued, but RS2 conviction is capped unless diversification beyond its largest customer becomes more visible.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

CRUS is exposed to smartphone unit cycles, premium-device demand, content gains, and semiconductor inventory timing. High-end consumer electronics are cyclical, though product refresh cycles and content expansion can offset unit weakness.

Rates matter through valuation, but less than for unprofitable tech because CRUS is already profitable and cash rich. China demand, supply-chain localization, and consumer electronics tariffs/policy can also influence results.

Macro conclusion: strong balance sheet offsets some cycle risk, but revenue is still tied to device demand.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for supplier companies with one dominant customer is tricky. They can be very profitable for long periods, but valuation must account for customer concentration, design-win risk, and bargaining power. The best outcomes occur when the supplier gains content per device and uses cash flow to diversify.

CRUS has produced record revenue and strong FCF [Actual], which improves the base rate. But concentration risk prevents a high multiple. The market is correct to assign a discount versus more diversified analog peers.

Base-rate score: positive if bought at a reasonable multiple.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Cirrus has strong mixed-signal engineering capability, high gross margins, and a debt-free balance sheet. Q4 FY2026 gross margin was 53.0% on both GAAP and non-GAAP bases [Actual]. That is attractive semiconductor economics.

The weakness is customer concentration. If CRUS loses content, misses a product cycle, or faces pricing pressure at its largest customer, earnings can move quickly. Diversification into power, sensing, and adjacent mixed-signal applications is important but still must be proven.

Quality score: 7.5/10. Strong financial model and balance sheet, offset by concentration.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Cash-rich balance sheets create second-order value through buybacks. CRUS repurchased $280 million in FY2026 and still ended with about $1.2 billion of cash and investments and no debt [Actual]. If shares remain reasonably priced, buybacks can support EPS.

The negative second-order effect is bargaining power. A large customer can influence pricing, timing, and specifications. Even without losing a socket, margin pressure can reduce the value of design wins.

## SECTION 4. Scenarios

Bear case: smartphone weakness or content loss pushes EPS toward $7.00 and the multiple stays low. Fair value around $110 to $125 [Estimate].

Base case: FY2026 earnings prove sustainable, buybacks reduce share count, and diversification modestly improves. Fair value $170 to $185 [Estimate].

Bull case: content gains and diversification drive EPS above $11.00, while cash piles up. Fair value exceeds $220 [Estimate].

Probability-weighted view: positive, but with customer-concentration risk.

Best horizon: 2 to 4 years.
