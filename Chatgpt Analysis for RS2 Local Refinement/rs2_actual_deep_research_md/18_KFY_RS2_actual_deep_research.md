## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Korn Ferry is a global organizational consulting, executive search, professional search, interim, digital, and recruitment process outsourcing company. The market often treats it like a cyclical executive-search name, but the company has become a more diversified human-capital advisory platform with recurring or backlog-like elements in Digital and RPO, a large installed base of enterprise relationships, and a very strong cash-rich balance sheet.

The FY2026 setup is favorable. Korn Ferry reported FY2026 fee revenue of $2.907 billion, up 7% reported and 5% constant currency; total revenue of $2.939 billion; net income attributable to Korn Ferry of $277.4 million; diluted EPS of $5.22; adjusted diluted EPS of $5.28; and adjusted EBITDA of $497.8 million at a 17.1% margin. Estimated remaining fees under existing contracts were $1.883 billion, up 10% year over year. Q1 FY2027 guidance calls for fee revenue of $725 million to $745 million and diluted EPS of $1.32 to $1.38.

At $69.68, KFY trades around 13.2x FY2026 adjusted EPS and about 7.3x market-cap-to-adjusted EBITDA. However, the company had $1.381 billion of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities at April 30, 2026, or $728.9 million net of amounts held in trust for deferred compensation plans and accrued bonuses, against $398.6 million of long-term debt. Using the more conservative available-net-cash figure, enterprise value is roughly $3.3 billion, or about 6.6x FY2026 adjusted EBITDA. Using gross cash and securities, EV is materially lower. Either way, the balance sheet gives KFY more downside protection than the headline P/E suggests.

Final view: KFY is an attractive, shareholder-friendly, moderately cyclical business trading at a reasonable valuation with a better mix than its historical executive-search label implies. The stock is not a no-risk compounder because hiring cycles can roll over quickly, but the current price offers a good risk/reward for staged accumulation.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Korn Ferry is tied to corporate confidence, white-collar hiring, executive turnover, transformation budgets, M&A, restructuring, and enterprise spending on leadership, assessment, rewards, and workforce design. It is not a pure staffing business, but the cycle matters.

The current macro backdrop is mixed:

- Enterprise cost discipline remains important after several years of wage inflation and higher rates.
- AI and automation are forcing companies to rethink roles, skills, leadership models, and workforce architecture.
- Executive hiring and professional search can improve when confidence rises, but can slow quickly if CEOs freeze hiring.
- Consulting budgets are scrutinized, though human-capital transformation can remain funded if it is linked to productivity, AI adoption, and operating-model redesign.
- Global exposure creates currency and regional economic sensitivity.

KFY's FY2026 results show resilience. Fee revenue grew 7% reported and 5% constant currency, despite a still uneven white-collar environment. Professional Search & Interim grew 11%, Executive Search grew 9%, Consulting and RPO each grew roughly 4%, and Digital was flat. That is not explosive growth, but it is a credible recovery profile for a cyclical advisory firm.

Macro conclusion: favorable enough. KFY is leveraged to a gradual corporate-confidence recovery and to AI/workforce redesign demand, but it remains exposed to a hiring downturn.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for executive search and human-capital consulting is cyclical. These companies can produce excellent returns when bought at low multiples near cycle troughs, but earnings can compress quickly when hiring freezes spread. The strongest firms have global brands, deep client relationships, data assets, repeatable consulting offerings, and enough balance-sheet flexibility to buy back stock during downturns.

KFY's multi-year pattern:

- FY2024 total revenue: $2.796 billion; diluted EPS: $3.23.
- FY2025 total revenue: $2.761 billion; diluted EPS: $4.60.
- FY2026 total revenue: $2.939 billion; diluted EPS: $5.22.

That pattern suggests a company exiting the post-2022 slowdown with margin improvement and better mix, not a company in structural decline. Adjusted EBITDA grew from $463.9 million in FY2025 to $497.8 million in FY2026 while adjusted EBITDA margin stayed near 17%.

Base-rate positives:

- Strong global brand in executive search and leadership advisory.
- Growing mix beyond traditional search.
- High free-cash-flow conversion in normal years.
- Cash-rich balance sheet.
- Meaningful dividends and buybacks.
- Share count reduction over time.

Base-rate negatives:

- Revenue can be cyclical and confidence-sensitive.
- Consultant productivity matters.
- High compensation expense limits operating leverage.
- Digital is not yet a high-growth SaaS-like segment.
- Professional search/interim can be more competitive than elite executive search.

Base-rate conclusion: KFY should be underwritten as a cyclical advisory platform with above-average balance-sheet quality, not as a pure recurring software or defensive professional-services company.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Korn Ferry operates across five solution areas:

- Consulting: organization strategy, assessment and succession, leadership development, rewards, and talent strategy.
- Digital: technology, data, platform, AI capabilities, and subscription/license offerings around Korn Ferry's talent assets.
- Executive Search: board, C-suite, and senior executive recruitment, reported by region.
- Professional Search & Interim: permanent professional placement and interim talent.
- RPO: outsourced recruitment programs, often supported by larger contracts.

FY2026 fee revenue by major area:

- Consulting: $691.7 million.
- Digital: $363.5 million.
- Executive Search: $924.1 million.
- Professional Search & Interim: $561.1 million.
- RPO: $367.1 million.

The business is more diversified than a simple "headhunter" label. Executive Search is still the largest contributor, but Consulting, Digital, Professional Search & Interim, and RPO together are larger. That matters because a more integrated offering can deepen client relationships and reduce dependence on one hiring cycle.

Quality strengths:

- Global brand and reputation.
- Large enterprise client relationships.
- Broad talent data and assessment assets.
- Diversified service mix.
- Strong adjusted EBITDA margins for a professional-services firm.
- High cash generation.
- Conservative balance sheet.
- Shareholder returns via dividends and buybacks.

Quality weaknesses:

- Human-capital advisory remains labor-intensive.
- Compensation expense is structurally high.
- Revenue can decline in recessions or hiring freezes.
- Digital is margin-rich but flat in FY2026.
- AI could both create consulting demand and compress parts of search/recruiting.
- Organic growth may remain mid-single-digit rather than high-growth.

Business quality score: solid. KFY is not a flawless compounder, but it is a better business than the market multiple implies.

## SECTION 2.5. Adjusted Financials

FY2026 reported and adjusted results:

- Fee revenue: $2.907 billion, up 6.5%.
- Total revenue: $2.939 billion, up 6.4%.
- Net income attributable to Korn Ferry: $277.4 million, up from $246.1 million.
- Diluted EPS: $5.22, up from $4.60.
- Adjusted diluted EPS: $5.28, up from $4.88.
- Adjusted EBITDA: $497.8 million, up from $463.9 million.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin: 17.1%.
- Operating income: $374.7 million, up from $346.3 million.
- Operating cash flow: $414.2 million.
- Capex: $89.9 million.
- Free cash flow before acquisitions: approximately $324.3 million.

Balance sheet:

- Cash and cash equivalents plus marketable securities: $1.3815 billion.
- Cash and securities net of trust/deferred-comp and accrued-bonus amounts: $728.9 million.
- Long-term debt: $398.6 million net of issuance costs.
- Total stockholders' equity: $1.979 billion.
- Working capital: $924.0 million.

Capital returns:

- FY2026 dividends paid to shareholders: $104.6 million.
- FY2026 stock repurchases: $115.7 million cash-flow statement basis; the 10-K also notes approximately $116.1 million repurchased.
- Q4 FY2026 repurchases: 1.24 million shares for $78.8 million.
- September 2025 board increase: $250 million, bringing available repurchase capacity to $331.4 million at that time.

Quality of earnings:

The adjusted EPS bridge is modest. FY2026 adjusted diluted EPS of $5.28 is close to GAAP diluted EPS of $5.22. Adjusted EBITDA excludes items such as integration/acquisition costs, impairments, restructuring, management separation charges, and a gain on modification of an office lease. FY2026 also had $13.8 million of accelerated depreciation on the Digital platform due to a platform sunset decision. The adjustment load does not look abusive.

Cash-flow conversion is good, but some cash is linked to deferred compensation and bonus obligations. For valuation, the more conservative approach is to treat only the $728.9 million net figure as readily available excess liquidity.

Adjusted financial conclusion: KFY is financially strong, cash-generative, and materially underlevered.

## SECTION 3. Valuation

Reference valuation at $69.68:

- Market cap: approximately $3.65 billion.
- FY2026 GAAP P/E: 13.3x.
- FY2026 adjusted P/E: 13.2x.
- Gross-cash-adjusted EV: roughly $2.7 billion.
- Conservative available-cash-adjusted EV: roughly $3.3 billion.
- EV/FY2026 adjusted EBITDA: roughly 5.4x to 6.6x depending on cash treatment.
- Free cash flow yield on market cap: roughly 8.9% using FY2026 FCF before acquisitions.
- Dividend yield: approximately 2.7% to 2.9% depending on forward dividend run rate.
- Price/book: about 1.85x.

KFY is not optically distressed, but the valuation is reasonable for a company with net cash, high FCF conversion, and growing fee revenue. The market appears to price it like a mature cyclical search company rather than a broader talent/advisory platform.

Base case valuation:

- Normalized adjusted EPS: $5.30 to $5.80.
- Fair multiple: 14x to 16x.
- Base fair value: $74 to $93.

Bull case valuation:

- EPS power: $6.25 to $7.00 if fee revenue grows mid-single digits and margin stays near 17% to 18%.
- Fair multiple: 16x to 18x.
- Bull value: $100 to $126.

Bear case valuation:

- EPS falls to $3.75 to $4.25 in a hiring slowdown.
- Fair multiple: 10x to 12x.
- Bear value: $38 to $51, partly cushioned by net cash and buybacks.

Valuation conclusion: attractive but cyclical. The current price offers moderate upside and a cash-backed downside buffer, but not immunity from a hiring recession.

## SECTION 4. Scenarios

Bull scenario:

Corporate confidence improves, Executive Search continues to recover, Professional Search & Interim remains strong, Consulting benefits from AI/workforce transformation, and Digital returns to growth. Fee revenue compounds 5% to 7%, adjusted EBITDA margin expands toward 18%, and adjusted EPS reaches $6.50 to $7.00. With a 16x to 18x multiple, the stock can reach $105 to $125.

Base scenario:

Fee revenue grows low-to-mid single digits, Digital remains stable, RPO and Consulting grow modestly, adjusted EBITDA margin stays around 17%, and buybacks reduce shares. Adjusted EPS grows toward $5.50 to $5.90. At 14x to 16x, fair value is $77 to $94.

Bear scenario:

Hiring slows, executive search weakens, professional search demand fades, and consulting budgets tighten. Fee revenue declines mid-to-high single digits, adjusted EBITDA margin drops below 15%, and EPS falls to $3.75 to $4.25. The stock could trade down to $40 to $50.

Stress scenario:

A global recession or white-collar hiring freeze hits across search, professional staffing, RPO, and consulting simultaneously. KFY remains solvent and liquid, but earnings contract sharply. The balance sheet becomes the thesis until revenue recovers.

Scenario conclusion: upside/downside is favorable enough for a staged buy, but position sizing must acknowledge cyclical drawdown risk.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

The market's short-term focus is likely to be Q1 FY2027 guidance, hiring-cycle signals, and whether FY2026 growth continues. The longer-horizon opportunity is that KFY may be building a more durable talent platform than investors credit.

What the market may miss:

- KFY is now a multi-solution human-capital platform, not just executive search.
- Estimated remaining fees under existing contracts were up 10% year over year.
- Cash and securities provide strategic optionality.
- Buybacks are meaningful at this valuation.
- AI disruption can create advisory work even while disrupting parts of recruiting.

What the market may overestimate:

- Digital as a software-like business. It was flat in FY2026 despite high margins.
- Margin expansion in a compensation-heavy professional-services model.
- The reliability of search revenue during downturns.

Horizon conclusion: the best underwriting horizon is 2 to 3 years. KFY should be judged by whether it can sustain mid-single-digit fee revenue growth, hold 17%+ adjusted EBITDA margin, and reduce share count through the cycle.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

The bearish behavioral trap is anchoring on KFY's old identity as an executive-search cyclical and ignoring the broader platform and balance sheet. The bullish behavioral trap is treating it like a recurring software/AI transformation winner and paying too high a multiple.

Investor biases to watch:

- Label bias: "headhunter" instead of diversified human-capital platform.
- Net-cash neglect: ignoring the balance sheet.
- Cycle blindness: assuming FY2026 growth will continue through any macro environment.
- AI narrative overreach: assuming workforce transformation demand automatically creates high-growth margins.
- Dividend/buyback complacency: treating capital returns as guaranteed even if earnings fall.

Behavioral rule: buy when valuation is at or below 13x normalized EPS and EV/EBITDA remains near mid-single digits; reduce if the stock rerates above 18x without faster organic growth.

## SECTION 7. Risks

Primary risks:

- Hiring-cycle downturn: executive search and professional search can contract quickly.
- Consulting-budget pressure: clients may defer discretionary transformation work.
- Digital stagnation: flat Digital fee revenue undermines the higher-multiple platform thesis.
- AI disruption: automation may reduce pricing power in parts of recruiting and assessment.
- Compensation leverage: employee compensation absorbs a large share of revenue.
- International exposure: currency and regional weakness can affect results.
- Deferred-comp/cash complexity: gross cash is not all excess cash.
- Acquisition risk: KFY may use cash for acquisitions that dilute returns.
- Talent retention risk: consultants and search professionals are key assets.
- Reputational risk: executive search and advisory work depends on trust.

Most important risk: a macro/hiring slowdown arriving just as investors begin to extrapolate FY2026's recovery.

## SECTION 9. Red Team

The strongest bear argument:

KFY is a cyclical professional-services company near a recovery point. FY2026 benefited from improving search and professional placement demand, but a recession or hiring freeze could cut earnings quickly. Digital is not growing, and high compensation costs prevent the company from scaling like software. The net-cash argument is overstated because deferred-comp and bonus-related cash is not fully discretionary. A 13x multiple may be fair, not cheap, if EPS is near a cyclical high.

The strongest bull rebuttal:

KFY is not expensive even using conservative cash treatment, adjusted earnings are clean, FCF conversion is strong, and the balance sheet is excellent. The company is buying back stock, paying dividends, and guiding to solid Q1 FY2027 EPS. The platform is more diversified than traditional executive search, and AI/workforce transformation should create demand for exactly the kind of organization design, leadership, assessment, and skills work KFY sells.

My synthesis:

The bear case is valid on cyclicality, but the current price already discounts a meaningful amount of cyclicality. The bull case wins because the valuation is moderate, the balance sheet is strong, and the business mix is better than the market label.
