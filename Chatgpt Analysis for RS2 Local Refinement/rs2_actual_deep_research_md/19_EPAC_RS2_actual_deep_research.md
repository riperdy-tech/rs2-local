## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Enerpac Tool Group is a pure-play industrial tools and solutions company focused on high-pressure hydraulic tools, controlled force products, heavy lifting, and related services. It is a niche industrial with high gross margins, strong brand/channel position, disciplined cost controls, and a shareholder-return orientation. The company has transformed itself into a cleaner, higher-margin business since becoming a pure-play industrial tools platform.

The current stock setup is good-business / fair-price rather than obvious bargain. FY2025 was strong: net sales were $616.9 million, adjusted EBITDA was $153.6 million, adjusted EBITDA margin was 24.9%, adjusted diluted EPS was $1.81, operating cash flow was $111.3 million, and the company returned about $69 million through repurchases. But fiscal 2026 has been mixed through Q2. First-half FY2026 net sales were $299.0 million, up from $290.7 million; adjusted diluted EPS was $0.75 versus $0.79; adjusted EBITDA was $65.3 million versus $68.1 million; and Q2 gross margin declined 410 bps year over year to 46.4% due to service-business pressure.

Management narrowed FY2026 guidance after Q2 to net sales of $635 million to $650 million, organic sales growth of 1% to 3%, adjusted EBITDA of $158 million to $163 million, adjusted EPS of $1.85 to $1.92, and free cash flow of $100 million to $110 million. At $34.87, EPAC trades at roughly 18.4x to 18.8x FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance and about 11.7x to 12.1x EV/FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, using Q2 net debt of $88.5 million. That is not excessive for a high-quality niche industrial, but it does not leave much room for service weakness or tariff/macro pressure.

Final view: EPAC belongs on the RS2 RN list, but the action is not aggressive buying before Q3. The company reports fiscal Q3 2026 results after the market close on July 7, 2026. I would wait for that print or demand a better entry below $31.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Enerpac sells into industrial, oil and gas, energy, power generation, infrastructure, commercial construction, truck, automotive, and heavy-lifting applications. Demand is tied to maintenance, repair, capital projects, industrial production, large infrastructure jobs, and customers' willingness to spend on safe, reliable tooling.

Current macro setup:

- Industrial activity is uneven, with product demand healthier than service demand.
- EMEA service softness is a clear pressure point.
- Middle East conflict risk can affect service work, logistics, and customer timing.
- Tariffs and trade restrictions remain relevant for global industrial supply chains.
- Higher interest rates can delay capital projects, but maintenance and safety-critical tools are more resilient than discretionary machinery.
- Infrastructure and energy project demand can provide offsets to general industrial softness.

EPAC's Q1 and Q2 FY2026 data show the split clearly:

- Q1 FY2026 net sales declined 1%; product sales within IT&S grew 4% organically, while service revenue declined 26% year over year.
- Q2 FY2026 net sales rose 6%; IT&S product sales grew 6% organically, while service revenue declined 17% organically.

Macro conclusion: product demand appears stable-to-healthy, but service softness, especially EMEA, reduces confidence in near-term margin expansion.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Niche industrial tool companies with strong brands can be excellent businesses when they have durable distribution, high gross margins, aftermarket/service exposure, and disciplined capital allocation. However, they are still cyclical industrials. When activity slows, volumes and service utilization weaken, and operating leverage can work against them.

EPAC's recent base rate:

- FY2024 net sales: $589.5 million.
- FY2025 net sales: $616.9 million.
- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA: $153.6 million.
- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin: 24.9%.
- FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS: $1.81.
- FY2026 guidance: adjusted EPS $1.85 to $1.92 and adjusted EBITDA $158 million to $163 million.

This is a good base rate: modest organic growth, high margins, cash generation, and buybacks. But it is not high growth. The company is guiding to only 1% to 3% organic growth in FY2026, and first-half adjusted EBITDA declined year over year despite higher sales.

Base-rate conclusion: good niche industrial, not a secular grower. It deserves an above-average industrial multiple, but not an unlimited one.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Enerpac's core Industrial Tools & Services segment sells high-pressure hydraulic tools, controlled force products, and services used in demanding industrial applications. The company also owns Cortland Biomedical, a smaller but faster-growing business. Enerpac's value proposition is safety, precision, reliability, and application expertise in jobs where tool failure or poor execution can be costly.

Quality strengths:

- Recognized global brand in specialized industrial tools.
- High gross margin profile.
- Strong adjusted EBITDA margin near 25% in FY2025.
- Low net leverage.
- Disciplined shareholder returns.
- Product demand has remained healthier than service demand.
- DTA acquisition adds horizontal moving capability and cross-selling potential.
- Cortland Biomedical is growing faster than core IT&S.

Quality weaknesses:

- Organic growth is modest.
- Service revenue can be volatile by region and project timing.
- EMEA weakness is currently material.
- Industrial distribution/channel performance matters.
- Exposure to tariffs, FX, commodities, and geopolitical disruptions.
- Buybacks can create value only if done below intrinsic value.

Business quality score: above average. EPAC is a focused, high-margin industrial franchise, but current growth is not strong enough to justify chasing the stock.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

The behavioral trap is paying a premium multiple for a high-quality industrial because the FY2025 margin profile looked excellent, while ignoring the first-half FY2026 deterioration. The opposite trap is dismissing EPAC because of one weak service cycle, even though product demand and balance-sheet quality remain solid.

Biases to monitor:

- Quality bias: assuming high margins mean low risk.
- Authorization bias: overvaluing the $200 million buyback authorization before seeing execution price and timing.
- Event-risk neglect: buying days before Q3 despite already fair valuation.
- Cyclical extrapolation: treating service softness as permanent or treating FY2025 margins as permanent.

Behavioral rule: do not make a large pre-Q3 bet. Let the next report resolve the service-margin question.
