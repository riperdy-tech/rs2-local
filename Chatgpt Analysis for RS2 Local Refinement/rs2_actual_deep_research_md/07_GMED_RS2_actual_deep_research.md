## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Globus Medical is a musculoskeletal technology company focused on spine, orthopedic trauma, joint reconstruction, biomaterials, enabling technologies, robotic/navigation systems, neuromonitoring, and spinal cord stimulation after the Nevro acquisition. It has evolved from a highly profitable spine implant company into a broader orthopedic/spine procedural platform.

GMED is one of the cleaner RS2 candidates so far. The stock is not dirt cheap, but the setup has several qualities the screen is likely rewarding:

- Q1 2026 revenue growth of 27.0%;
- base business growth excluding Nevro of 13.2%, or 11.2% constant currency;
- adjusted EBITDA margin of 32.3%;
- Q1 non-GAAP EPS growth of 64.7%;
- full-year 2026 revenue guidance reaffirmed at $3.18-$3.22 billion;
- full-year 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance raised to $4.70-$4.80;
- $799.3 million of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities at March 31, 2026;
- no traditional long-term debt listed in the Q1 balance sheet;
- continuing synergy and operating leverage from NuVasive and Nevro integration.

At $80.12, GMED trades at about 16.9x the midpoint of 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance and roughly 16.5x if using the high end. Adjusting for net cash/marketable securities, enterprise value is about $10.27 billion, or about 3.2x 2026 guided revenue. Q1 adjusted EBITDA annualizes to nearly $981 million, although that overstates the full-year baseline if seasonality or integration timing shifts; even so, the multiple is not demanding for a high-quality medtech platform growing faster than many peers.

The key risk is that the headline growth includes Nevro contribution and integration/synergy benefits. The bear case is that 2026 is an unusually favorable post-acquisition year and growth normalizes faster than the current multiple assumes. The bull case is that Globus has turned the NuVasive acquisition from a feared integration risk into a scale advantage and is now converting that scale into share gains, margin expansion, and a stronger salesforce.

Final view: **Accumulate / Buy in stages**. GMED has a better risk/reward than many high-score names because valuation is still reasonable relative to execution. Preferred entry is below $78, but current price is acceptable for a starter.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

GMED sits in the medical device and musculoskeletal surgery ecosystem. The macro drivers are more durable than most cyclical sectors:

- aging population;
- degenerative spine conditions;
- orthopedic trauma;
- growing procedural volumes;
- surgeon demand for navigation, robotics, implants, and workflow efficiency;
- hospital and ASC pressure to improve outcomes and reduce revision risk.

The macro headwinds are also real:

- hospital capital budgets can constrain robotics and enabling technology placements;
- procedure volumes are sensitive to staffing, reimbursement, and site-of-care economics;
- device pricing can face purchasing pressure from hospitals and group purchasing organizations;
- medical device companies face intense sales-rep competition;
- FDA, EU MDR, quality systems, recalls, and product liability are constant risks.

The spine market is competitive and historically prone to pricing pressure, but Globus has a differentiated profile because it pairs implants with enabling technology. ExcelsiusGPS, navigation, imaging, neuromonitoring, biologics, and now spinal cord stimulation create an ecosystem rather than a pure commodity implant catalog.

Macro verdict: **positive but competitive**. Demand is secularly durable, but pricing and execution matter.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Medtech companies with strong product cadence and surgeon relationships often compound well, but acquisitions can destroy value when salesforce disruption, culture mismatch, product overlap, or inventory/channel issues emerge. The NuVasive deal was initially controversial for exactly that reason. Nevro added another integration layer in 2025.

The base rate says:

- pure-play spine companies can be volatile;
- medtech rollups often promise synergies that take longer than expected;
- salesforce disruption is a frequent problem after large transactions;
- product cycles matter more than accounting EPS;
- balance sheet strength matters because R&D and acquisition opportunities are recurring.

Globus has so far beaten that base rate. Full-year 2025 revenue grew 16.7%, Q4 2025 revenue grew 25.7%, Q1 2026 revenue grew 27.0%, and base business excluding Nevro still grew double digits in Q1. US Spine posted its third straight quarter of 10% growth, according to management's Q1 commentary.

However, the base-rate caution should remain. If Nevro stabilization stalls, if salesforce turnover rises, or if NuVasive integration synergy peaks in 2026, the market could stop capitalizing adjusted EPS at a medtech quality multiple.

Base-rate verdict: **better-than-average execution against a historically tricky integration base rate**.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Globus sells into two major product categories:

- Musculoskeletal Solutions: implants, biologics, surgical instruments, spinal cord stimulation, neuromonitoring, and related spine/orthopedic/neurosurgical products.
- Enabling Technologies: robotic navigation, imaging, workflow, and digital surgical tools.

Q1 2026 product-category sales:

- Musculoskeletal Solutions: $733.0 million.
- Enabling Technologies: $26.9 million.
- Total net sales: $759.9 million.

Enabling Technologies is still a smaller revenue category, but it can have strategic importance beyond its revenue line. Robotics/navigation tools can support implant pull-through, surgeon loyalty, procedural standardization, and account penetration. The risk is that capital equipment cycles are lumpier than implant consumption.

Business strengths:

- broad product portfolio across spine and musculoskeletal procedures;
- strong US Spine momentum;
- high gross margin structure;
- technology-enabled ecosystem rather than commodity-only implants;
- international growth;
- significant cash and marketable securities;
- operating leverage from acquired scale.

Business weaknesses:

- high dependence on surgeon adoption and salesforce quality;
- intense competition from Medtronic, Stryker, Zimmer Biomet, Johnson & Johnson/DePuy Synthes, and smaller spine specialists;
- integration complexity from NuVasive and Nevro;
- regulatory and product-liability exposure;
- reimbursement and hospital-budget sensitivity;
- potential margin normalization after synergy capture.

Business-quality score: **8.5/10**.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

1. **NuVasive integration may be changing the company's scale economics.** The market originally feared sales disruption. Instead, US Spine is showing repeated 10% growth, and margins are expanding.

2. **Nevro could be more optionality than burden if stabilized.** Nevro added $82.7 million of Q1 2026 sales. The key is whether Globus can move from stabilization to growth without diluting margins.

3. **Enabling Technologies are strategically more valuable than their revenue contribution.** Robotics/navigation can drive implant adoption, improve surgeon workflow, and defend accounts.

4. **Net cash changes the risk profile.** GMED has meaningful cash and marketable securities with no traditional long-term debt in the Q1 balance sheet. That gives management flexibility for buybacks, R&D, distributor investment, and smaller tuck-ins.

5. **Adjusted EPS may understate or overstate true earnings depending on integration permanence.** If restructuring and acquisition costs fade, non-GAAP becomes more representative. If acquisitions remain a constant growth mechanism, some exclusions are recurring economic costs.

Second-order verdict: **positive, with integration durability as the deciding variable.**

## SECTION 7. Risks

**1. Integration risk**

NuVasive and Nevro integration remain central. Systems, salesforces, culture, product portfolios, and customer relationships must be maintained.

**2. Salesforce risk**

The 10-K highlights reliance on direct sales representatives and independent distributors. Turnover or competitor poaching can disrupt sales.

**3. Competitive risk**

The spine and musculoskeletal device market is intensely competitive, with large diversified medtech competitors and specialized spine companies.

**4. Pricing and reimbursement**

Hospitals, payors, and group purchasing organizations can pressure pricing. Procedure economics can shift.

**5. Regulatory risk**

Medical devices require FDA, EU MDR, quality system, labeling, advertising, tissue/biologics, and international compliance.

**6. Product liability and recalls**

Implants and surgical technologies can create liability if outcomes disappoint or defects occur.

**7. Non-GAAP adjustment risk**

Amortization, integration costs, restructuring costs, and contingent consideration changes can obscure true economics.

**8. Enabling Technologies lumpiness**

Robotic/navigation systems may be strategic but can produce uneven quarterly revenue.

Risk rating: **medium**.
