## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Blue Bird is a school bus manufacturer benefiting from fleet replacement, low-emission and electric bus demand, pricing power, and operational improvement. Fiscal Q2 2026 net sales were $352.6 million to $353 million, GAAP net income was $29.3 million, adjusted EBITDA was $50.8 million to $51 million, adjusted EBITDA margin was 14.4%, and 2,148 buses were sold [Actual]. Management raised FY2026 guidance to about $1.75 billion revenue and about $245 million adjusted EBITDA [Actual].

At $76.08, BLBD has a $2.47 billion market cap, P/E of 18.7x, and EPS of $4.07 [Actual]. Against guided adjusted EBITDA of $245 million, market cap / EBITDA is about 10.1x before net debt adjustment [Estimate]. That is reasonable if margins hold and the company keeps executing.

Core view: Buy, but cyclical. BLBD has strong momentum and a clear margin story, yet school bus demand, government funding, and tariffs need monitoring.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

School buses are tied to municipal budgets, school district replacement cycles, state/federal funding, and financing conditions. EV and low-emission incentives can accelerate demand, but funding delays can create lumpiness.

Tariffs and supply-chain costs are relevant. Blue Bird explicitly noted managing and responding to tariff impacts in its Q2 deck [Actual]. Higher input costs can pressure margins if pricing cannot keep up.

Macro conclusion: demand tailwinds are good, but funding and cost cycles matter.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for cyclical vehicle manufacturers is mixed. They can look cheap near peak margins and expensive near trough margins. The positive difference for Blue Bird is a specific fleet/EV transition and margin-improvement story.

Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.4% and raised FY2026 guidance support the bull case [Actual]. The base-rate warning is that bus production, district funding, and EV demand can be cyclical.

Base-rate score: positive but cyclical.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Blue Bird has a long operating history, school-bus brand recognition, and leadership in electric and low-emission buses. Q2 backlog was about 3.6 thousand units valued at $646 million, including 912 EVs valued at $287 million [Actual]. Alternative power sales mix was 41% [Actual].

The company is not asset-light software; manufacturing execution, working capital, labor, and supply chain matter. The Micro Bird acquisition broadens the platform but adds integration risk.

Quality score: 6.5/10. Good niche franchise, but manufacturing cyclicality limits the score.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

EV adoption can create second-order benefits through service, grants expertise, and district relationships. If Blue Bird becomes the default low-emission school-bus partner, backlog quality improves.

The negative second-order effect is funding dependency. EV orders can be grant-driven, and government program timing can create volatility.

## SECTION 4. Scenarios

Bear case: funding delays, tariff costs, or production issues compress EBITDA. Fair value below $55 [Estimate].

Base case: FY2026 guidance is achieved and margins remain mid-teens. Fair value $75 to $85 [Estimate].

Bull case: long-term $2.5 billion revenue / 15%+ margin target becomes credible. Fair value above $110 [Estimate].

Probability-weighted view: positive.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

Short-term investors focus on quarterly bus deliveries. Long-term investors should focus on backlog, alternative-power mix, and margin durability. The market may underappreciate structural fleet modernization if it focuses only on manufacturing cyclicality.

Best horizon: 2 to 4 years.
