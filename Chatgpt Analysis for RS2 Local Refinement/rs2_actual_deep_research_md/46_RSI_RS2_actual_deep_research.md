## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Online gaming demand has some discretionary characteristics, but iGaming and sports betting are also shaped by legalization, product availability, and marketing economics. Macro weakness can pressure consumer deposits, but state-by-state legalization and online migration can offset ordinary cyclicality.

Interest rates matter mainly through valuation. Profitable growth companies with long runways are worth more when discount rates fall. If rates remain high, RSI needs to keep proving operating leverage to justify its multiple.

Regulatory macro is more important than GDP macro. Tax rates, marketing restrictions, responsible-gaming rules, and licensing decisions can materially change unit economics. The U.S. iGaming market remains underpenetrated relative to sports betting, so legalization optionality is a long-term tailwind [Estimate].

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for online gambling operators is bifurcated. Winners with strong retention, disciplined marketing, and favorable market access can scale profitably. Weaker operators can grow revenue while destroying value through promotions, high customer acquisition costs, and tax leakage.

RSI's Q1 2026 data moves it toward the winner group: revenue growth of 41%, net income growth of 134%, and adjusted EBITDA growth of 81% show operating leverage [Actual]. However, base rates warn against extrapolating early profitability too far. Competitive intensity can return quickly if larger peers spend more aggressively.

Base-rate score: positive but not fully proven. The model is validating, but the category remains regulatory and competitive.
