## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Betterware de México, now presenting as BeFra, is a Mexico-focused consumer platform built around Betterware home goods and Jafra beauty/personal care. It has a direct-selling model, high dividend yield, and improving profitability, but it also carries Mexico consumer risk, debt, FX exposure, internal-control weaknesses, and integration risk around the pending Tupperware LatAm transaction.

Q1 2026 consolidated results were mixed at the top line but strong in profitability. Net revenue was Ps.3,509.7M [Actual], up only 0.3% year over year according to StockTitan summary [Actual/Estimate], while EBITDA increased 13.9% to Ps.609.9M [Actual], with a 17.4% margin [Actual]. Net income increased 86.7% to Ps.281.4M [Actual], and free cash flow was Ps.351.5M [Actual]. Net debt / EBITDA improved to 1.50x [Actual].

FY2025 context shows a modest-growth but profitable business. Revenue was approximately Ps.14.24B [Actual/Estimate], up 1.0%, while earnings increased 49.0% to approximately Ps.1.06B [Actual/Estimate]. The 2025 20-F reports outstanding indebtedness of Ps.4.464B [Actual/Estimate from filing summary]. Management expects 2026 revenue of Ps.14.8B-Ps.15.4B and EBITDA margin of at least 19%, excluding the pending Tupperware LatAm transaction [Actual].

At approximately $18/share and market cap around $670M-$676M [Actual], BWMX trades around 10x trailing earnings [Actual/Estimate] with a dividend yield around 6.7% [Actual/Estimate]. That is attractive if execution and governance improve. The discount is partly justified by material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting identified in the 2025 20-F [Actual/Estimate from filing summary].

RS2 conclusion: Speculative Buy. BWMX offers value, yield, margin improvement, and deleveraging. It should be sized modestly because internal controls, Mexico macro, Tupperware transaction risk, and direct-selling cyclicality are real.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

BWMX is tied to Mexican household consumption, direct selling activity, disposable income, FX, and interest rates. Mexico consumer demand has been uneven, and management/third-party summaries note subdued consumption trends in recent 2025 quarters [Actual/Estimate].

Direct selling can be resilient when consumers seek extra income, but it can also weaken when consumer confidence and purchasing power fall. Representative/distributor motivation, catalog quality, product innovation, and logistics execution are critical.

FX matters. BWMX reports in Mexican pesos, trades in U.S. dollars, and sources a meaningful portion of product supply from China according to 20-F summaries [Actual/Estimate]. Peso/USD and peso/CNY movements can affect margins and USD investor returns.

The pending Tupperware LatAm transaction is a macro/strategic overlay. It can expand scale and categories, but it can also add integration complexity, working-capital needs, brand turnaround risk, and leverage pressure.

Macro score: mixed. Valuation compensates for some risk, but Mexico consumer and FX exposures require a discount.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Base rate for direct-selling businesses: high cash returns are possible, but growth can be volatile due to distributor churn, consumer cycles, catalog fatigue, and reputational risk.

Base rate for high-dividend emerging-market consumer stocks: yields can be attractive, but payouts may vary with FX, leverage, and capital needs.

Base rate for acquisitions of distressed/legacy consumer brands: upside exists if distribution and product refresh work, but integration can distract management and consume cash.

Base rate for companies with material internal-control weaknesses: investors should demand a valuation discount until remediation is proven.

Base-rate conclusion: the low multiple is not accidental. BWMX can work, but only with strict monitoring of leverage, controls, cash flow, and dividend coverage.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

BWMX's core business quality is moderate. Betterware and Jafra have recognizable brands, direct-selling networks, catalog/product innovation, and local market knowledge. The model can generate strong EBITDA and cash when volumes and mix are favorable.

Q1 2026 margin improvement is the best quality signal. EBITDA rose 13.9% even with nearly flat revenue [Actual], showing cost/mix discipline. ROIC improved to 27.0% [Actual], suggesting better capital efficiency.

The dividend/yield is a quality and risk signal. A 6.7% dividend yield [Actual/Estimate] is attractive, but payouts must be tested against free cash flow and acquisition needs.

Business-quality concerns are material. The 2025 20-F identified material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting, including business combination accounting, period-end reporting/consolidation, and IT general controls [Actual/Estimate]. These do not mean the numbers are wrong, but they reduce confidence.

Business quality score: 8.5/15. Strong profitability and cash returns are offset by direct-selling cyclicality, control weaknesses, and acquisition risk.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Margin recovery has second-order deleveraging benefits. Higher EBITDA reduces net debt/EBITDA and creates more flexibility for dividends and integration.

Internal-control weaknesses have second-order multiple effects. Even if operations improve, investors may not pay a higher multiple until remediation is complete.

The Tupperware LatAm transaction has second-order brand and logistics effects. If successful, it can expand the platform; if poorly integrated, it can distract management and consume cash.

Dividend yield has second-order investor support. Income investors can support the stock, but a dividend cut would hurt sentiment disproportionately.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

The market may also be rationally discounting the stock because governance and control remediation take time. The horizon edge exists but requires patience and monitoring.

Best setup: own a modest position while collecting dividend, add only after evidence of control remediation and integration success.
