## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

AZZ is a North American metal-coatings platform focused on hot-dip galvanizing and coil coating. It is not a commodity steel producer. The company earns money by extending the useful life, corrosion resistance, and appearance of customers' steel and metal products. That makes the business more service/process-oriented and margin-resilient than a simple steel-volume story.

Fiscal 2026 was strong. AZZ reported total sales of $1.650 billion, up 4.6%, adjusted EBITDA of $367.6 million, adjusted EBITDA margin of 22.3%, adjusted EPS of $6.19, and GAAP EPS of $10.50. Metal Coatings sales increased 14.1% to $758.7 million, while Precoat Metals sales declined 2.3% to $891.4 million. Segment adjusted EBITDA margins were 31.0% in Metal Coatings and 19.8% in Precoat Metals.

The balance sheet improvement is a major thesis pillar. AZZ reduced debt by $385.3 million during fiscal 2026 and lowered net leverage to 1.4x from 2.5x. Operating cash flow was $525.4 million, though this included $273.2 million of distributions from the AVAIL joint venture. Capex was $80.8 million, dividends were $23.1 million, and repurchases were $20.0 million.

The problem is valuation and forward growth cadence. At about $150-$152 per share, AZZ trades around a $4.5 billion market cap and $5.0 billion enterprise value. Against FY2027 guidance for $360-$400 million of adjusted EBITDA and $6.50-$7.00 adjusted EPS, the stock trades at roughly 13x FY2027 EV/EBITDA and 22x guided adjusted EPS. That is not absurd for a high-quality industrial compounder, but it leaves less room for disappointment in Precoat volumes, construction demand, zinc/natural gas/input costs, or M&A execution.

RS2 summary: AZZ is a high-quality industrial with real operating momentum and a much cleaner balance sheet, but the stock has already recognized much of the improvement. It is a watchlist buy, not a chase. Preferred entry is on a pullback or after FY2027 Q1 confirms stronger organic growth and margin durability.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

AZZ's macro exposure sits at the intersection of infrastructure, construction, industrial production, steel fabrication, HVAC, transportation, and metal building products. The backdrop is constructive but uneven.

Infrastructure demand supports the Metal Coatings segment. Management said Q4 FY2026 Metal Coatings sales rose 25.7% year over year, primarily due to increased infrastructure-related demand and easier weather comparisons. U.S. infrastructure spending and industrial reshoring themes remain supportive for galvanized steel used in bridges, transmission, utility, transportation, and industrial structures.

Construction data is mixed. Dodge reported total construction starts rebounded in spring 2026, including a 34% May increase and earlier gains in April and March. But U.S. Census May 2026 construction spending showed total construction spending only 0.1% above April and 1.5% below May 2025, with first-five-month spending down 2.7% year over year. That suggests project starts and spending are not uniformly strong.

Precoat Metals remains more cyclical. AZZ said FY2026 Precoat sales fell 2.3% because of materially lower volume in residential construction, HVAC, and transportation end markets, partially offset by pricing. That segment can pressure consolidated growth if interest rates, housing, transportation, or HVAC demand stay soft.

Input macro matters. Hot-dip galvanizing uses zinc and natural gas, while coil coating uses paint and related materials. Inflation, tariffs, labor availability, and energy volatility can affect margins if AZZ cannot pass costs through quickly enough.

Macro score: moderately positive for Metal Coatings, mixed for Precoat, and not strong enough to justify chasing at any price.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Base rate for specialty industrial compounders: companies with leading share, localized service networks, recurring customer relationships, process know-how, and pricing discipline can compound well for long periods. AZZ fits much of this profile.

Base rate for roll-up/acquisition industrials: value creation depends on disciplined purchase prices, integration, safety, plant utilization, and debt control. AZZ's debt reduction after prior strategic activity improves confidence, but M&A remains a risk area.

Base rate for highly cyclical building-product suppliers: margins can look best near mid-cycle/high-cycle conditions. Investors often overpay when backlog, pricing, and operating leverage all look strong. AZZ is less cyclical than commodity producers but not immune.

Base rate for leverage cleanup stories: the best returns often occur before leverage normalization is fully visible. With net leverage already down to 1.4x and the stock near its 52-week high, part of that re-rating has already happened.

Base-rate conclusion: quality is real; entry discipline matters. The stock should not be treated as a distressed rerating opportunity anymore.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

AZZ describes itself as the leading independent provider of hot-dip galvanizing and coil coating solutions in North America. The company has two core operating segments:

- Metal Coatings: hot-dip galvanizing and related corrosion-protection services.
- Precoat Metals: coil coating and prepainted metal solutions for construction, appliances, HVAC, transportation, and other end markets.

Metal Coatings is the crown jewel. FY2026 sales increased 14.1% to $758.7 million, and adjusted EBITDA was $235.5 million, a 31.0% margin. Q4 FY2026 Metal Coatings sales rose 25.7% and adjusted EBITDA rose 30.1%, with a 30.2% margin. That points to strong utilization, pricing, and infrastructure demand.

Precoat Metals is valuable but currently softer. FY2026 sales fell 2.3% to $891.4 million, and adjusted EBITDA was $176.2 million, a 19.8% margin. The margin profile is respectable, but lower volume in residential construction, HVAC, and transportation demonstrates cyclicality.

Durability comes from customer need. Corrosion protection is not decorative fluff; it extends asset life and lowers maintenance costs. The American Galvanizers Association emphasizes hot-dip galvanizing's long useful life and use in harsh environments and infrastructure. That supports recurring demand across replacement, repair, and new-build cycles.

AZZ also has operational proof. Fiscal 2026 marked its 39th consecutive year of profitability from continuing operations. That kind of record matters in industrials because it shows management has navigated multiple cycles.

Business quality score: 12/15. AZZ has leadership, process know-how, high-margin Metal Coatings, and improving capital allocation. The deductions are Precoat cyclicality, input-cost exposure, and acquisition/integration history.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Debt paydown has second-order benefits. Lower leverage reduces interest expense, increases strategic flexibility, and can make the equity more acceptable to generalist investors. FY2027 guidance already reflects lower expected interest expense, so part of the benefit is visible.

Infrastructure demand has a second-order margin effect. Higher volumes through galvanizing plants can raise utilization and spread fixed costs, which explains why Metal Coatings margins can move above 30%. If infrastructure demand remains strong, incremental margins could surprise positively.

Precoat weakness has the opposite second-order effect. Lower residential/HVAC/transportation volumes can pressure absorption and make pricing harder. If Precoat remains soft while Metal Coatings normalizes, consolidated margins may plateau.

Capex has a timing effect. The Washington, Missouri plant is expected to be accretive in FY2027, and capital spending is guided at $80-$100 million. Growth capex can build long-term capacity but may temporarily suppress free cash flow.

Input costs are second-order through customer behavior. If zinc, paint, energy, or tariff-driven costs rise, AZZ may pass through some cost, but customers may delay projects if total project economics worsen.

Metal Coatings continues double-digit growth, infrastructure demand remains robust, Precoat volumes stabilize, the Washington facility contributes, and FY2027 adjusted EBITDA exceeds $400 million. Debt reduction continues, interest expense falls, and the market values AZZ at 13x-15x EBITDA or mid-20s EPS. Upside could reach $170-$190 over 12-24 months.

Base case:

The stock is fairly valued around $140-$155 and returns come mostly from modest earnings growth rather than multiple expansion.

Bear case:

Infrastructure demand normalizes, Precoat volumes remain weak, input costs rise, and FY2027 adjusted EBITDA lands near or below $360 million. At 10x-11x EBITDA, equity value could fall toward $110-$125.

Stress case:

A construction/industrial downturn hits both Metal Coatings and Precoat while fixed costs, labor, and input volatility remain elevated. Debt is manageable but no longer shrinking as quickly. The stock could revisit the low-$90s to low-$100s range, especially if investors rotate away from industrial cyclicals.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

The long-term edge in AZZ is that the market may underappreciate the durability of corrosion-protection demand and the value of a North American coatings footprint. Infrastructure, grid hardening, transportation, utilities, and industrial maintenance are multi-year demand pools.

The short-term issue is that the market may already be correctly appreciating this. The stock has appreciated sharply and is near its 52-week high. With FY2027 EBITDA guidance only slightly above FY2026, the next leg likely requires either guidance upside or confidence that $400 million-plus adjusted EBITDA is sustainable.

Horizon arbitrage exists if an investor can wait for a cyclical air pocket. AZZ is the sort of company worth owning at the right price, but the better edge is likely patience rather than immediate action.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

The bullish behavioral error is extrapolating the stock chart and treating recent outperformance as proof of undervaluation. AZZ has executed well, but the valuation has already moved.

The bearish behavioral error is dismissing the company as a cyclical steel-adjacent name. AZZ is a specialty coatings/service company with differentiated margins, not a commodity steel mill.

The accounting trap is focusing on GAAP EPS of $10.50 without adjusting for AVAIL-related gains. Adjusted EPS of $6.19 in FY2026 and $6.50-$7.00 guidance for FY2027 are better measures of ongoing earnings power.

The cash-flow trap is capitalizing FY2026 operating cash flow without backing out the $273.2 million AVAIL distribution. Underlying cash generation is still strong, but not as explosive as the headline figure.

Behavioral rule: do not chase after a 50%+ one-year run. Put AZZ on a high-quality watchlist and demand either a price pullback or new evidence that guidance is conservative.

## SECTION 7. Risks

Precoat volume risk: residential construction, HVAC, and transportation weakness already pressured FY2026 Precoat sales. Further weakness could offset Metal Coatings strength.

Infrastructure timing risk: project delays, permitting, fiscal-policy changes, or a slowdown in public spending can reduce demand for galvanized steel.

Input-cost risk: zinc, natural gas, paint, labor, and transportation costs can compress margins.

Tariff/regulatory risk: tariffs can affect customer costs, supply chains, and pricing behavior.

Leverage risk: leverage is much improved, but AZZ still has roughly $515 million consolidated indebtedness and minimal cash.

Acquisition risk: management includes strategic M&A in capital allocation. Poorly priced deals could dilute returns or re-lever the balance sheet.

Non-GAAP risk: adjusted EPS adds back intangible amortization and other items. The metric is useful but must not replace cash analysis.

Cycle risk: industrials often look best when margins are high and demand is favorable. Buying at peak sentiment can impair returns.

Event risk: FY2027 Q1 results are scheduled for July 9, 2026, shortly after this report date. A miss or guide-down would likely hit the stock.
