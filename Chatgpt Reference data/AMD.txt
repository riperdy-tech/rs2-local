## Section 3.5. Second-Order Effects

**Competitive response:**
Nvidia will defend share through Blackwell/Vera/Grace systems, CUDA lock-in, NVLink ecosystem, and hyperscaler allocation priority. Intel remains a CPU competitor but less central to the AI GPU thesis. Custom ASICs from Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, and Broadcom partners may absorb inference workloads that AMD otherwise could win.

**Second-order risk:** hyperscalers may use AMD primarily as a bargaining tool against Nvidia. If AMD wins volume but at lower margin, revenue growth will not translate into shareholder value.

**Reflexivity:**
Positive: rising stock price helps retain talent and makes dilution less painful.
Negative: the same rising price creates high expectations; any shipment delay or gross margin miss can cause a severe multiple contraction.

**Customer behavior evolution:**
Early hyperscaler deployments may be strategic and subsidized by collaboration economics. Sustained repeat orders require AMD to prove total cost of ownership, software reliability, cluster stability, and inference throughput at production scale.

**Network dynamics:**
AMD benefits from open ecosystem preference, but Nvidia still has the strongest full-stack moat. This is not yet a winner-takes-most outcome for AMD.
