## Section 3.5. Second-Order Effects

**Competitive response:**
Bank of America, Citi, Wells Fargo, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Capital One, fintechs, private credit, and asset managers compete across JPM’s businesses. JPM’s scale advantage is real, but private credit and fintech can pressure lending economics and fee pools.

**Reflexivity:**
Positive: a high stock price supports buybacks, talent retention, acquisition currency, and client confidence.
Negative: bank stocks can derate quickly if markets fear credit losses or regulatory capital constraints.

**Customer behavior evolution:**
Deposit betas and funding costs are the key behavioral variable. If customers demand higher deposit yields or shift cash into money-market funds, NII pressure increases.

**Network dynamics:**
The payments, card, treasury services, and institutional client network are scale-reinforcing. JPM’s moat widens if more clients consolidate banking relationships with the strongest counterparty.
