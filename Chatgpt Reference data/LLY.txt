## Section 3.5. Second-Order Effects

**Competitive response:**
Novo Nordisk remains Lilly’s most important competitor, but Lilly has gained momentum in GLP-1 injectables and oral GLP-1 development. Amgen, Roche, Pfizer, Viking, and many smaller biotech competitors remain relevant, especially in next-generation obesity and cardiometabolic assets.

**Reflexivity:**
Positive: Lilly’s high stock price helps finance acquisitions, attract talent, and build manufacturing capacity.
Negative: if GLP-1 growth slows, the stock can compress sharply because the valuation assumes durable category leadership.

**Customer behavior evolution:**
The market is moving from supply-constrained early adopters toward broader payer-covered and cash-pay populations. That expands TAM but increases pricing and access sensitivity.

**Network dynamics:**
No true network effect. Lilly’s moat comes from patents, clinical data, regulatory approvals, manufacturing scale, physician familiarity, and payer access.
