**Baseline Date:** June 26, 2026

Palantir reported revenue of **$1.633B**, GAAP operating income of **$754M**, GAAP net income attributable to common stockholders of **$871M**, adjusted operating income of **$984M**, cash from operations of **$899M**, and adjusted FCF of **$925M**. ([SEC][1])
**Tpf:** current valuation reflects Palantir’s raised 2026 guidance: revenue of **$7.650–$7.662B**, adjusted operating income of **$4.440–$4.452B**, adjusted FCF of **$4.2–$4.4B**, and U.S. commercial revenue growth of at least **120%**. ([SEC][1])

**Archetype:** **B/D hybrid — AI software quality compounder + expectation-driven growth stock**
**Valuation Engine:** **Engine 3 / Engine 4 hybrid:** platform-quality valuation with expectation-pressure stress test.

**Why this engine:** PLTR is now profitable, cash-generative, and scaling fast, so it is not a pure speculative software name. But the valuation is so high that the stock must still be judged by reverse expectations: how much future AI-platform dominance is already priced in?

## Section 1. Macro Context

PLTR benefits from three powerful forces: enterprise AI adoption, U.S. government AI/defense modernization, and the need for secure AI workflows in regulated environments. It is also exposed to software multiple compression, AI competition, government procurement timing, geopolitical controversy, and the risk that customers build internal AI tooling instead of expanding Palantir. The company itself highlights risks around AI use, macro conditions, tariffs/trade relations, contract realization, customer retention, and long/unpredictable sales cycles. ([SEC][1])

**Macro alignment:** **Positive for fundamentals, negative for valuation discipline.**
The AI demand wave is real, but the stock already carries a premium AI-infrastructure multiple.

## Section 2.5. Adjusted Financials

The key adjustment is **stock-based compensation**. PLTR’s Q1 adjusted operating income excludes **$201.6M** of stock-based compensation and **$28.0M** of related payroll taxes. That is acceptable for tracking operating momentum, but RS2 still treats SBC as a real shareholder cost because it can dilute owners over time. ([SEC][1])

| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------: |

| Q1 adjusted net income                    |      **$856.5M** `[Actual]` |
| Q1 SBC                                    |      **$201.6M** `[Actual]` |
| Q1 adjusted FCF                           |      **$924.6M** `[Actual]` |
| FY26 adjusted FCF guide midpoint          |     **~$4.3B** `[Estimate]` |
| FY26 SBC-adjusted owner earnings proxy    | **~$3.4–3.6B** `[Estimate]` |
| Diluted shares                            |      **~2.571B** `[Actual]` |

PLTR’s diluted weighted-average share count was **2.571B** in Q1 2026 versus **2.553B** in Q1 2025, so dilution was limited recently, but the absolute SBC expense remains meaningful. ([SEC][1])

**Adjusted financials judgment:** high quality, but use both adjusted FCF and SBC-adjusted owner earnings.

## Section 7. Risks & Catalysts

### Top 3 Risks

1. **Valuation compression:** even after the decline, PLTR trades around **35x FY26 revenue** and **62x adjusted FCF**.
2. **Growth deceleration:** Q1 growth is extraordinary, but the base rate for sustaining **70%+ revenue growth** at increasing scale is poor.
3. **Government/political/customer risk:** Palantir’s contracts can include termination-for-convenience provisions, and the company notes that RDV and TCV assume options are exercised and contracts are not terminated. ([SEC][1])

### Top 3 Catalysts

1. U.S. commercial growth remains near or above the **120%** 2026 guide.
2. U.S. government AI/defense work continues scaling; Reuters noted demand tied to AI in modern warfare and Maven becoming a Pentagon program of record. ([Reuters][4])
3. Adjusted FCF margin remains above **50%**, proving PLTR is not sacrificing profitability for growth.

## Section 9. Red Team

### Short Thesis

1. PLTR is a great business, but **120x reported earnings** and **~35x forward revenue** are still very demanding. ([SEC][1])
2. Q1’s **85% revenue growth** and **60% adjusted operating margin** are exceptional, but investors may be extrapolating peak acceleration too far. ([SEC][1])
3. Adjusted metrics exclude stock-based compensation; Q1 SBC was **$201.6M**, or about **12% of revenue**. ([SEC][1])
4. The top three customers accounted for **16%** of FY25 revenue, so concentration is lower than feared but still relevant. ([SEC][2])
5. Government work can be politically controversial and procurement-dependent.
6. AI competition is intensifying from cloud providers, defense primes, consultants, model companies, and internal enterprise teams.
7. The stock’s upside depends on PLTR becoming a category-defining AI operating system, not just continuing to be a very good software company.
