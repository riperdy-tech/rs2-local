## Section 0. Analysis Overview

**Company / Ticker:** Meta Platforms / META
**Baseline Date:** June 25, 2026
**T0:** current price **$552.22**, market cap **$1.416T**, P/E **~20.1x**, EPS **$27.50** `[Actual]`.
**T-1:** latest reported financials: FY26 Q1, quarter ended March 31, 2026. Meta reported Q1 revenue of **$56.31B**, operating income of **$22.87B**, operating margin of **41%**, net income of **$26.77B**, and diluted EPS of **$10.44**; however, Q1 EPS included a large tax benefit, and excluding it EPS would have been **$3.13 lower**. ([메타][1])
**Tpf:** current price reflects Q1 2026 results and the higher 2026 capex outlook of **$125–145B**. ([메타][1])

**RS2 classification:** **B. Quality Compounder with AI/Reality Labs reinvestment drag**
**Valuation Engine:** **Engine 1 — Traditional DCF + Multiples**, with explicit capex and Reality Labs drag adjustment.

**Why this engine:**

1. Meta’s core Family of Apps business is already highly profitable: FY25 FoA operating income was **$102.47B**, while Q1 FY26 FoA operating income was **$26.90B**. ([메타][2])
2. Revenue growth remains strong at scale: Q1 FY26 revenue grew **33% YoY**, with ad impressions up **19%** and average price per ad up **12%**. ([메타][1])
3. The company is not binary or pre-profit; it is an established cash-generative platform, even though AI and Reality Labs create major reinvestment uncertainty.

**Transition status:** Not a pure transition company. META is a quality compounder with high reinvestment intensity. Engine 4 would overstate AI optionality; Engine 1 with explicit capex stress is cleaner.

**Model Confidence:** **Medium-High**. Core advertising data is strong; long-term AI capex ROI and Reality Labs economics remain `[Assumption]`.

## Section 3.5. Second-Order Effects

**Competitive response:**
Google/YouTube, TikTok, Amazon Ads, Apple, Snap, and retail media networks will keep competing for ad budgets. TikTok remains the biggest attention competitor; Amazon Ads is the biggest commerce-intent competitor.

**Reflexivity:**
Positive stock-price reflexivity helps Meta retain AI talent through SBC and gives management freedom to invest. Negative reflexivity appears if AI capex is viewed as uncontrolled, because the market can rerate META from a high-quality ad compounder into a capital-intensive AI infrastructure spender.

**Customer evolution:**
Advertisers are increasingly buying automated creative, AI targeting, and performance outcomes rather than manual campaign tools. This helps Meta if AI improves ROAS, but hurts if ad inventory growth outruns advertiser demand.

**Network dynamics:**
The core network remains strong across Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, Messenger, and Threads. WhatsApp monetization remains underdeveloped optionality.

3. Capex guidance stabilizes or future capex intensity declines after the 2026 buildout.

**Highest-impact bear scenario:** Meta becomes a capital-intensive AI infrastructure spender while the incremental monetization accrues slowly, causing FCF compression and multiple derating.

## Section 9. Red Team

### Short Thesis

1. META’s reported P/E understates true risk because Q1 EPS had an unusually large tax benefit.
2. The company is guiding to **$125–145B** of 2026 capex, which can crush FCF conversion.
3. Reality Labs remains a multi-year drag with limited revenue.
4. Mark Zuckerberg’s voting control lowers outside shareholder governance leverage. Meta discloses that Class B shares have ten votes per share and that Class B holders can control stockholder approvals while Class B remains above the required voting threshold. ([SEC][3])
5. TikTok, YouTube, Amazon Ads, and Apple privacy/regulation can pressure ad growth.
6. AI recommendation and creative tools may become table stakes rather than a durable pricing advantage.
7. The stock is not hated; bullish options positioning limits contrarian upside.
