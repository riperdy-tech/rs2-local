## 2. Business quality

GE Vernova sells and services gas, nuclear, hydro, wind, grid, power conversion, storage, and electrification software equipment across Power, Wind, and Electrification. Power is anchored by long-cycle generation and service agreements, Electrification sells grid equipment and software, and Wind remains the weak segment. The investment thesis is a backlog-to-free-cash-flow conversion thesis, not a pure AI narrative.

## 3.5. Second-order effects

| Competitor | Likely response | Thesis impact |
|---|---|---:|
| Siemens Energy | Compete hard in gas services, grid, and wind restructuring | -1 |
| Eaton / Schneider / Vertiv / Hitachi Energy | Compete in electrification, grid, data-center power chain | -1 |
| Mitsubishi / large gas turbine competitors | Compete for gas turbine slots and long-term service agreements | -1 |

Second-order risk is not immediate price war risk; it is capacity, labor, supply-chain, and margin normalization risk. If customers become less willing to pay scarcity pricing for turbine slots or grid equipment, today’s backlog margin accretion could fade.

Positive reflexivity exists: high share price supports employee retention, M&A currency, and confidence with utility customers. Negative reflexivity matters less because the company has cash, backlog, and low debt.

## 9. Red team

### Short thesis

1. GEV is a high-quality business priced like a best-case compounder before proving a full independent cycle.
2. At ~40x 2026 FCF, the stock has little tolerance for Wind losses, order normalization, tariffs, or rate pressure.
3. FY2026 guidance is strong, but the market is already underwriting 2028–2032 execution.
4. Wind remains a material negative segment and could absorb management attention and cash.
5. Power and Electrification margins may be near scarcity-cycle highs.
6. Buybacks at elevated prices could become value destructive.
7. Crowding and institutional ownership raise downside volatility if momentum reverses.
