## Section 1. Macro Context

CEG benefits from three strong macro forces: electricity demand growth, clean/reliable-power scarcity, and AI/data-center load growth. Reuters reported that North American data-center power needs are projected to rise from **31 GW in 2025 to 66 GW in 2027**, which explains why power assets have become strategic infrastructure. ([Reuters][3])

The offset is that CEG is more exposed to market power prices than a regulated utility. Q1 reference prices show why earnings can swing: PJM West power rose to **$97.16/MWh** from **$53.69/MWh**, NYISO Central rose to **$112.23/MWh** from **$75.31/MWh**, and PJM capacity prices rose sharply versus the prior year. ([SEC][1])

**Macro alignment:** **Positive, but volatile.** CEG is a direct beneficiary of U.S. power scarcity, but the stock can derate if power prices, capacity prices, or policy support normalize.

## Section 3.5. Second-Order Effects

**Competitive response:** NEE, CEG, Vistra, NRG, Duke, Southern, Entergy, IPPs, private infrastructure funds, hyperscaler-backed power developers, and gas turbine suppliers are all competing to serve data-center load. The bottleneck is not only power generation; it is interconnection, transmission, permits, fuel, dispatchability, reliability, and contract structure.

**Reflexivity:** Higher power prices make CEG’s assets more valuable and can fund reinvestment, uprates, debt reduction, and buybacks. But if customers or regulators perceive excessive profits, political intervention risk rises.

**Customer behavior:** Hyperscalers and large corporates increasingly want firm, clean, 24/7 power rather than generic renewable certificates. That is exactly where CEG’s nuclear fleet is most valuable.

**Network dynamics:** No classic software network effect, but CEG’s customer platform matters. The company’s Q1 release notes a **380 MW** agreement with CyrusOne at Freestone and an exclusive Phase 2 opportunity for an additional **380 MW**. ([SEC][1])

## Section 7. Risks & Catalysts

### Top 3 Risks

1. **Power-price and capacity-market normalization:** Q1 power and capacity prices were very strong in PJM and NYISO; if these normalize, earnings expectations can compress. ([SEC][1])
2. **Calpine integration / leverage risk:** Calpine materially expands gas exposure, debt, and operating complexity. The acquisition materials expected investment-grade ratings and a return to target credit metrics by year-end 2027, but that requires execution. ([SEC][5])
3. **Regulatory/political risk:** if data centers, power prices, or capacity-market reforms become politically sensitive, CEG’s scarcity economics could be capped.

### Top 3 Catalysts

1. Additional long-term nuclear or clean-firm-power PPAs with hyperscalers, retailers, or industrial customers.
2. Calpine integration proves accretive and helps serve data-center load with firm power.
3. Nuclear uprates, license renewals, and the Crane restart advance on schedule.

**Highest-impact bear scenario:** CEG remains operationally excellent, but power-market regulation and lower forward prices drive a multiple reset from “scarce AI-power compounder” toward “merchant IPP.”
