## Section 3.5. Second-Order Effects

**Competitive response:**
EOG, Devon, Permian Resources, ConocoPhillips, Occidental, Exxon, Chevron, and private operators compete for services, acreage, labor, rigs, and completion crews. The main second-order risk is that higher oil prices trigger U.S. shale supply growth, weakening future WTI and narrowing FCF upside.

**Reflexivity:**
Positive: lower share count through buybacks raises oil production per share and FCF per share if executed below intrinsic value.
Negative: if oil prices fall, the stock can drop, buybacks may slow, and leverage perception worsens.

**Customer behavior evolution:**
FANG sells commodities, so it has no customer loyalty moat. The key “customer” behavior is global demand and refinery demand, not direct end-customer retention.

**Network dynamics:**
No network effect. Value creation depends on acreage quality, drilling execution, capital allocation, and commodity price discipline.

## Section 7. Risks & Catalysts

### Top 3 Risks

1. **WTI downside:** If WTI falls below **$60/bbl**, FCF and buyback capacity compress quickly.
2. **Service-cost inflation:** higher rig/completion activity can raise well costs and reduce FCF sensitivity.
3. **Basin concentration:** nearly all core operations are Permian/Midland Basin focused, increasing geographic and regulatory concentration risk.

### Top 3 Catalysts

1. WTI remains near or above **$70/bbl** while FANG executes the raised production guide.
2. Buybacks continue below intrinsic value, increasing FCF/share and production/share.
3. Debt falls toward management’s preferred lower range, improving equity value and shareholder-return flexibility.

**Highest-impact bear scenario:** WTI falls to the mid-$50s while FANG has already increased activity, leaving higher capex and lower FCF at the same time.
