## Section 2. Business Quality

American Tower owns and leases communications infrastructure: towers, rooftop sites, distributed antenna systems, and data centers. Its customers include wireless carriers, broadcasters, cloud/hybrid-cloud customers, and enterprises. The business is understandable: tenants lease mission-critical vertical real estate, and adding tenants to an existing tower is high-margin.

## Section 3.5. Second-Order Effects

**Competitive response:** Crown Castle and SBA Communications are the closest U.S. tower peers, while data-center competition includes Equinix and Digital Realty. AMT’s tower business has strong barriers; its data-center business faces more capex-heavy competition.

**Reflexivity:** A lower stock price makes equity issuance less attractive, so balance-sheet improvement must come from AFFO retention, asset recycling, and disciplined capex. Positive reflexivity appears if rates fall and the stock re-rates, improving capital-market flexibility.

**Customer behavior:** Carrier consolidation and capex cycles can create churn or slower leasing, but mobile data growth pushes networks toward densification over time.

**Network dynamics:** Towers are not a pure network effect, but site density and zoning scarcity create local quasi-monopolies. Data-center interconnection can create stronger network effects, but AMT is not yet in EQIX’s category.

2. **Slow AFFO growth:** 2026 AFFO/share guide implies only **2.1%** midpoint growth. ([American Tower][1])
3. **U.S. tower softness:** 2026 U.S. & Canada property revenue guidance implies a **-3.0%** midpoint growth rate, partly due lower straight-line revenue recognition. ([American Tower][1])

### Top 3 Catalysts

1. Rates fall or credit spreads tighten, helping REIT multiples.
2. Data-center property revenue grows toward the **12.5%** guidance midpoint. ([American Tower][1])
3. U.S. carrier leasing improves as 5G densification and future 6G prep require more network capacity.

**Highest-impact bear scenario:** Rates stay high, AFFO/share growth remains low, and AMT trades like a leveraged bond proxy instead of a digital-infrastructure compounder.
