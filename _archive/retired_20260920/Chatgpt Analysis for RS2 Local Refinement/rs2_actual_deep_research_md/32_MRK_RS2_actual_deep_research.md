## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Merck is a high-quality global pharmaceutical company, but the current investment question is not whether the business is excellent. The question is whether the stock near $130 [Actual] offers enough margin of safety before the Keytruda patent and IRA/pricing transition.

Q1 2026 sales were $16.286B [Actual], up 5% reported and 3% ex-FX [Actual]. KEYTRUDA/KEYTRUDA QLEX sales were $8.0B [Actual], up 12% reported and 8% ex-FX [Actual], including $128M of KEYTRUDA QLEX sales [Actual]. WINREVAIR sales were $525M [Actual], up 88% [Actual]. Animal Health sales were $1.8B [Actual], up 13% reported and 6% ex-FX [Actual]. These are strong operating facts.

The reported earnings picture is distorted by business-development charges. Q1 2026 GAAP loss per share was $(1.72) [Actual], and non-GAAP loss per share was $(1.28) [Actual], both including a $3.62/share charge related to the Cidara acquisition [Actual]. Full-year 2026 guidance calls for $65.8B-$67.0B sales [Actual] and $5.04-$5.16 non-GAAP EPS [Actual], also including the $3.62/share Cidara charge [Actual]. Excluding that charge mechanically implies roughly $8.66-$8.78 of underlying 2026 non-GAAP EPS before the separate Terns charge [Estimate]. The Terns acquisition was expected to add an approximate $2.35/share one-time charge and $0.12/share post-close EPS drag [Actual].

The fundamental risk is Keytruda concentration. In 2025, Keytruda represented 49% of Merck's total sales [Actual]. Merck states that Keytruda sales are expected to be materially negatively impacted by biosimilar competition between 2028 and 2029, with biosimilar competition potentially beginning in December 2028 when the primary compound patent expires [Actual]. Merck also expects Keytruda to be selected for IRA price setting in 2027, with material U.S. sales decline after the January 1, 2029 effective date [Actual].

RS2 conclusion: MRK is a Hold at current price. It is a strong business with credible post-Keytruda replacement assets, but the stock is near a 52-week high and the next several years carry unusual transition risk. Buy discipline matters. The better entry is below $115-$120 or after clearer evidence that WINREVAIR, KEYTRUDA QLEX, WELIREG, enlicitide, oncology ADCs, Cidara, Terns, and Animal Health can offset the Keytruda cliff.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Large-cap pharma has a favorable demand backdrop and an unfavorable pricing backdrop. Aging populations, oncology prevalence, cardiovascular/pulmonary disease, vaccine demand, and animal-health protein demand all support Merck's long-term revenue base. However, governments and payers are actively pressuring drug prices.

The U.S. IRA is central. Merck expects Keytruda to be included in a subsequent product selection for IRA price setting in 2027, with the selected price effective January 1, 2029 [Actual]. That timing overlaps with the expected biosimilar window, creating a double transition.

Vaccines are no longer a clean macro offset. Gardasil/Gardasil 9 sales fell 19% reported and 22% ex-FX in Q1 2026 to $1.069B [Actual]. Merck cited lower demand in China, lower Japan sales after a catch-up immunization program, and U.S. public-sector purchasing patterns [Actual]. The 2025 10-K says Gardasil/Gardasil 9 sales in China are not expected to materially increase in 2026 [Actual].

Animal Health is a stabilizer. Q1 2026 Animal Health sales were $1.8B [Actual], up 13% reported, supported by livestock and companion animal demand. This segment provides diversification from human pharma patent cycles, but it is not large enough to offset a Keytruda cliff by itself.

Macro score: mixed. The medical-demand base is strong; pricing and patent cliffs are the dominant macro risks.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Base rate for mega-cap pharma patent cliffs: markets often derate companies before major loss-of-exclusivity events unless replacement assets are highly visible. Keytruda is unusually large, so the burden of proof is high.

Base rate for pharma M&A before cliffs: late-stage acquisitions can create value, but large premiums and asset-acquisition accounting charges indicate Merck is paying up for replacement growth. Cidara and Terns may be strategically sensible, but not automatically value-accretive.

Base rate for oncology incumbents: oncology leaders can extend product life through combinations, earlier-line usage, subcutaneous forms, and new modalities. KEYTRUDA QLEX and combinations with WELIREG, Padcev, ADCs, and Daiichi Sankyo assets may soften but not eliminate cliff risk.

Base rate for vaccine softness: inventory/channel issues can reverse, but China-local competition and lower demand can persist. Gardasil should not be treated as a guaranteed rebound.

Base-rate conclusion: Merck is a quality transition story, not a simple compounder. The right base rate is "pharma cliff replacement," which deserves a valuation discount.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Merck's business quality remains high. It has global commercial scale, deep oncology expertise, major vaccine franchises, Animal Health diversification, strong R&D, and a long dividend history.

Keytruda is the core asset. 2025 Keytruda/Keytruda QLEX sales were $31.680B [Actual], up 7% [Actual], and Q1 2026 sales were $8.0B [Actual], up 12% [Actual]. The product continues to gain across metastatic and earlier-stage indications, including urothelial cancer, triple-negative breast cancer, cervical cancer, and renal cell carcinoma [Actual].

WINREVAIR is the most important new growth driver. 2025 sales were $1.443B [Actual], and Q1 2026 sales were $525M [Actual], up 88%. Merck presented positive Phase 2 CADENCE data in combined post- and precapillary pulmonary hypertension with HFpEF and plans registrational Phase 3 development [Actual]. This can expand the pulmonary hypertension opportunity, although Merck pays a 22% royalty on net sales to BMS [Actual].

Gardasil is high-quality but currently impaired. Q1 2026 sales declined sharply, and Merck has paused China shipments until inventory normalizes [Actual]. Local competition in China is a real strategic negative [Actual].

Animal Health is a valuable side engine. It generated $6.4B of 2025 sales [Actual] and $1.8B in Q1 2026 [Actual]. The segment's drivers are not identical to oncology or vaccines, adding diversification.

Pipeline quality is broad but expensive. Recent updates include KEYTRUDA QLEX, WELIREG combinations, ifinatamab deruxtecan priority review, enlicitide positive Phase 3 data, IDVYNSO approval, ENFLONSIA approval, and TERN-701 from Terns [Actual]. The problem is not lack of shots on goal; it is whether enough of them scale before Keytruda erosion.

Business quality score: 13/15. The franchise is world-class; the deduction is concentration and timing.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

KEYTRUDA QLEX can reduce administration burden and potentially defend franchise share, but it also introduces Halozyme litigation risk around subcutaneous pembrolizumab [Actual]. The second-order benefit is life-cycle management; the second-order risk is legal and royalty/settlement economics.

Cidara and Terns represent more than accounting charges. They signal that Merck is willing to use large balance-sheet capacity to buy late-stage assets before Keytruda erosion. If these assets work, Merck extends its growth runway. If they disappoint, Merck has converted financial flexibility into write-off risk.

WINREVAIR's CpcPH-HFpEF opportunity could be a major second-order expansion if Phase 3 confirms benefit. The 22% royalty to BMS [Actual] means gross sales are not fully equivalent to Merck-owned sales.

Gardasil China weakness has second-order implications for emerging-market vaccine confidence. A local Chinese nine-valent HPV vaccine approval [Actual] may reduce long-term pricing and volume expectations.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

The market may be too focused on the visible Keytruda cliff and too skeptical of Merck's replacement portfolio. That could create a multi-year opportunity if WINREVAIR, WELIREG, enlicitide, RSV, IDVYNSO, TERN-701, CD388, OHTUVAYRE, and Animal Health together build a credible post-2028 base.

At current price, however, the horizon arbitrage is not compelling enough. The stock has already benefited from defensive rotation and trades close to a 52-week high. Better risk/reward likely comes during a pullback or after a pipeline proof point.

Horizon conclusion: Merck is a watchlist compounder with a pending cliff. It is worth owning at the right price, but the price should compensate for uncertainty.
