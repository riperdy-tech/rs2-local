## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Docebo is a Canadian enterprise learning-management and learning-platform software company listed on Nasdaq and the TSX. It provides AI-enabled learning, training, content, analytics, external education, skills intelligence, and workforce-readiness functionality for enterprises, governments, and partner/customer education programs. The 365Talents acquisition extends the company from learning execution into skills intelligence and internal mobility.

The current setup is an asymmetric small-cap software value situation, but not a clean compounder. Q1 2026 total revenue grew 14.5% to $65.6 million, subscription revenue grew 11.9% to $60.6 million, ARR grew 10.6% to $248.9 million, adjusted EBITDA grew 23.2% to $11.0 million, adjusted EPS was $0.34 diluted, and free cash flow was $27.6 million. Management raised FY2026 guidance to total revenue of $271 million to $273 million, subscription revenue of $253.5 million to $255.5 million, and adjusted EBITDA of $54.5 million to $56.5 million.

At $18.24, DCBO trades at roughly 1.7x market-cap/FY2026 revenue, about 1.5x cash-adjusted EV/FY2026 revenue, about 7.2x cash-adjusted EV/FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, and around 10x to 11x current-year adjusted EPS estimates. Those are low multiples for a SaaS company with ~80% gross margin, positive FCF, and an enterprise AI learning narrative.

The reason it is cheap is also clear: ARR growth is only low double digit, FY2025 net dollar retention was 99%, the largest OEM customer is shrinking as a share of ARR, Q1 2026 reported a net loss under IFRS, working capital turned negative, and learning management is a competitive category. This is not a "pay any price" SaaS stock; it is a small-cap recovery/re-rating candidate.

Final view: DCBO is a Speculative Buy. The valuation is low enough to justify a starter position, but sizing should be modest until ARR growth reaccelerates above 13% to 15% excluding FX and the company proves that 365Talents and AI Workforce Readiness can lift NDR.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Docebo operates in enterprise learning, workforce development, customer education, partner enablement, compliance training, and skills management. Macro demand is tied to:

- Enterprise digital transformation.
- Reskilling and upskilling needs.
- AI-driven workforce change.
- Compliance training.
- Customer and partner certification programs.
- Public-sector workforce modernization.
- Distributed and hybrid work.

The macro backdrop is mixed. Companies need workforce readiness more than ever, especially as AI changes roles and required skills. But learning and talent software budgets can be scrutinized during slower hiring or cost-control periods. Many enterprises already own HCM or LMS modules from Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Cornerstone, Oracle, Microsoft-linked ecosystems, or specialized learning vendors, so Docebo must prove differentiation.

Q1 2026 customer examples support demand in higher-value enterprise use cases. Docebo cited a luxury hospitality and entertainment customer, a major U.S. financial services regulator, Databricks expanding with Docebo and 365Talents, and public-sector expansions with U.S. government and state customers. Those examples suggest Docebo is not limited to small-business training; it can win complex enterprise and government deals.

Macro conclusion: favorable enough, but competitive. AI workforce readiness is a real theme, but the category is crowded and buyer urgency varies.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for small-cap SaaS companies with low-teens ARR growth is mixed. If they can maintain high gross margins, expand EBITDA margins, and stabilize NDR, they can re-rate from very low multiples. If growth decelerates further, the market treats them like mature vertical software with limited upside.

Docebo's base-rate facts:

- FY2025 revenue: $242.7 million, up 11.9%.
- FY2025 subscription revenue: $228.4 million, up 11.8%.
- FY2025 ARR: $238.1 million, up 8.4%.
- FY2025 NDR: 99%.
- Q1 2026 ARR: $248.9 million, up 10.6%.
- Q1 2026 ARR excluding largest OEM and FX impact: up approximately 13.7%.
- Q1 2026 ACV: $71,000, up 25.9%.
- Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin: 16.8%.
- FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance implies roughly 20% margin.

The base rate is not elite, because NDR below 100% is a yellow flag. But the valuation is low and the margin trajectory is improving.

Base-rate conclusion: speculative GARP/value SaaS. Good if growth stabilizes; poor if ARR slows further.

Business quality score: decent but not pristine. Docebo is real software with cash generation, but the growth engine needs to reaccelerate.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

The bullish behavioral trap is buying because the multiple is low and ignoring why it is low. The bearish trap is dismissing DCBO because it is no longer a high-growth SaaS story while ignoring the improving EBITDA and FCF profile.

Biases to monitor:

- Low-multiple bias: assuming 1.5x revenue is automatically cheap.
- AI narrative bias: assuming "AI Workforce Readiness" creates a moat.
- FCF extrapolation bias: annualizing Q1's 42% FCF margin.
- NDR neglect: ignoring sub-100% net retention.
- Small-cap liquidity bias: underestimating volatility and spread risk.

Behavioral rule: size small until NDR improves and ARR growth stabilizes above low double digits.
