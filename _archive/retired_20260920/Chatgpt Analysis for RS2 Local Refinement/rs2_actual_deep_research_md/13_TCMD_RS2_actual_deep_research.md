## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Tactile Medical is a small-cap medtech company focused on at-home therapies for chronic conditions, primarily lymphedema and airway clearance. The investment case has improved materially: revenue growth reaccelerated, gross margin expanded, debt was repaid, the company is generating positive adjusted EBITDA, and management raised 2026 revenue guidance after Q1.

At $31.35, TCMD is not dirt cheap on headline GAAP earnings, but the enterprise value is only about $632 million after subtracting $75 million of cash and no debt. Against 2026 guidance of $360 million to $368 million of revenue and $49 million to $51 million of adjusted EBITDA, the stock trades around 1.7x forward sales and 12.6x forward adjusted EBITDA. For a company growing revenue around 9% to 12%, with 76% gross margins and improving operating leverage, that is reasonable.

The catch is risk. TCMD is not a large diversified medtech. It is exposed to reimbursement rules, Medicare prior authorization for pneumatic compression devices, payer documentation, sales-force productivity, patient access, and product-line concentration. The stock has already moved strongly. My conclusion is Speculative Buy / staged accumulate, not full-size buy. The setup is attractive, but this should remain a smaller satellite position.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Tactile sits in home-based medical therapy, a favorable long-term area because health systems and payers generally prefer effective at-home treatment when it can reduce complications, clinic visits, hospitalizations, or disease progression. Chronic lymphedema, lipedema-adjacent swelling management, venous/lymphatic disorders, and airway clearance needs are not economically cyclical in the usual sense.

The macro environment has two competing forces. On the positive side, aging populations, cancer survivorship, obesity-related comorbidities, chronic pulmonary conditions, and home-care adoption can support demand. On the negative side, payer scrutiny is increasing, Medicare documentation requirements are evolving, and prior authorization can affect timing and conversion.

Small-cap medtech also remains sensitive to investor risk appetite. TCMD can execute well and still trade weakly if small-cap healthcare sentiment deteriorates. The offset is that the balance sheet is clean and the business no longer depends on external capital.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for small-cap medtech companies is mixed. Many promising device companies struggle because reimbursement, sales efficiency, gross-to-net dynamics, and physician/patient adoption take longer than expected. The winners usually combine real clinical need, payer coverage, differentiated products, high gross margin, and improving sales productivity.

TCMD is moving toward the favorable side of that base-rate distribution. Q1 2026 revenue grew 23%, both product lines grew more than 20%, gross margin expanded to 76.5%, and adjusted EBITDA swung positive. Full-year 2026 guidance implies net income of roughly $25 million and adjusted EBITDA of about $50 million.

The base-rate caution is that one or two quarters do not prove durable compounding. TCMD's execution has improved, but the company must show that revenue growth is not merely a rebound from prior disruption and that Medicare prior authorization can be handled without slowing access.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Tactile Medical provides therapies for people with chronic disorders. The core franchise is lymphedema therapy, including Flexitouch and related compression systems. The smaller but faster-growing segment is airway clearance, including AffloVest.

The business model has attractive features. Products address chronic conditions, therapy is often used in the home, gross margins are high, and payer coverage/reimbursement capability can be a barrier to entry. The company also benefits from clinician referrals and patient support infrastructure. These are not simple consumer devices; reimbursement workflow and medical documentation matter.

Q1 2026 showed balanced growth. Lymphedema product revenue increased 23% to $62.2 million and represented 83% of total revenue. Airway clearance revenue increased 22% to $13.0 million and represented 17% of total revenue. The unchanged mix means the quarter was not driven by a single product-line anomaly.

The company also received FDA 510(k) clearance for its next-generation AffloVest airway clearance device and acquired LymphaTech to add 3D measurement and digital capabilities for lymphedema identification and care workflows. These are credible product and workflow enhancements.

Quality is good but not elite. TCMD has a focused niche and improving execution, but scale is limited and reimbursement dependency is high.

## SECTION 2.5. Adjusted Financials

Q1 2026 revenue was $75.3 million, up 23% from $61.3 million in Q1 2025. Gross profit was $57.6 million, up 27%, and gross margin improved to 76.5% from 74.0%. Operating expenses increased 19% to $59.1 million, but because gross profit grew faster, operating loss narrowed to $1.5 million from $4.5 million.

Net loss improved to $1.8 million, or $0.08 per share, from a net loss of $3.0 million, or $0.13 per share. Adjusted EBITDA improved to positive $3.7 million from negative $0.3 million. Q1 is seasonally not the strongest quarter, so the full-year guidance matters more than Q1 GAAP loss.

For 2025, total revenue was $329.5 million, up 12%, and adjusted EBITDA was $44.8 million. The company also generated GAAP net income of $19.1 million in 2025.

The balance sheet is clean. At March 31, 2026, TCMD had $75.0 million of cash and no outstanding borrowings under its credit agreement. Total current assets were $152.8 million, total liabilities were $54.3 million, and stockholders' equity was $217.9 million. The company repurchased $1.1 million of stock in Q1, with $23.9 million remaining under the repurchase program.

Management updated 2026 guidance to revenue of $360 million to $368 million, up 9% to 12% versus 2025, and maintained adjusted EBITDA guidance of $49 million to $51 million. GAAP net income guidance is $24.7 million to $26.1 million.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

First, the Medicare prior authorization environment is not purely negative. It creates operational burden, but TCMD's AI-enabled order management platform is designed to manage documentation and workflow. If the company handles prior auth better than smaller competitors or less organized providers, the rule change may become a relative advantage.

Second, sales-force productivity is a major swing factor. Management cited increased productivity from a fully resourced sales organization. If that productivity persists, revenue growth can outpace headcount growth and drive operating leverage.

Third, LymphaTech may matter beyond its small purchase price. Better 3D measurement and digital assessment can support objective diagnosis, patient identification, and workflow efficiency. That can improve referral conversion and long-term therapy adoption.

Fourth, airway clearance gives TCMD a second growth vector. It is still only 17% of revenue, but Q1 growth of 22% and the next-generation AffloVest clearance create room for continued expansion.

Fifth, buybacks are unusually relevant for a small-cap medtech if shares trade below intrinsic value. The remaining $23.9 million authorization equals roughly 3% of current market cap.

Adjusted EBITDA margin rises toward 16% to 18%, and the market rerates the stock to 15x to 17x EBITDA. Fair value: $45 to $50.

Stress case: Medicare reimbursement changes or prior authorization disruption materially reduce access to pneumatic compression devices. This would be a true thesis break because reimbursement is central to the model.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

The near-term market may focus on whether Q1 revenue strength was a one-off rebound or evidence of a durable acceleration. The stock can be volatile around Q2 because expectations have risen.

The longer-term investor can focus on a different question: can TCMD compound from a clean balance sheet, high gross margin, better sales productivity, and a larger portfolio? If yes, the current valuation is still acceptable.

The horizon arbitrage is that investors often avoid small-cap medtech due to reimbursement complexity. That complexity is real, but it may also be why TCMD trades at a reasonable multiple despite improving fundamentals.

The bearish trap is overreacting to the Q1 GAAP loss. Q1 showed improved gross margin and positive adjusted EBITDA, and full-year guidance implies GAAP net income. The quarter is better judged by revenue, gross margin, operating leverage, and full-year guidance than by the seasonal EPS print alone.

The disciplined view is to buy in stages. TCMD is attractive, but small-cap medtech deserves a risk discount.

## SECTION 7. Risks

Reimbursement risk is the largest risk. TCMD depends on payer coverage and documentation for pneumatic compression devices and related therapies. Medicare prior authorization could delay orders, increase administrative burden, or reduce conversion if not handled well.

Sales execution risk is meaningful. The company cited fully resourced sales organization productivity as a driver. If productivity falls, revenue growth and operating leverage can disappoint.

Product concentration risk is high. Lymphedema products were 83% of Q1 revenue. Airway clearance is growing, but it is still a smaller piece of the company.

Small-cap liquidity and multiple risk matter. TCMD's market cap is only about $707 million. Investor flows can amplify volatility.

Competition risk exists from other compression, vascular, lymphedema, and airway clearance therapy providers. Clinical differentiation and reimbursement support need to remain strong.

M&A integration risk exists after LymphaTech. The acquisition is small, but digital workflow assets must be integrated effectively to matter.

## SECTION 7.5. Correlation Risk

TCMD correlates with small-cap healthcare risk appetite, medtech reimbursement sentiment, and growth/margin execution. It is less correlated with large-cap technology or macro cyclicals, but in a risk-off small-cap tape it can decline regardless of fundamentals.

It also has payer-policy correlation. Any broad Medicare or DME reimbursement concern could pressure the stock even before company-specific impact is clear.

Portfolio implication: TCMD is a useful diversifier, but not a volatility reducer.
