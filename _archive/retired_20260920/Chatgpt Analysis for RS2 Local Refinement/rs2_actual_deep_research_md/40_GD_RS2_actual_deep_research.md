## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

General Dynamics is a major U.S. aerospace and defense contractor with four segments: Aerospace, Marine Systems, Combat Systems, and Technologies. Its portfolio combines Gulfstream business jets, nuclear submarines, combat vehicles, munitions, mission systems, and defense IT services. The business has high strategic relevance and very large backlog visibility.

Q1 2026 was strong. Revenue was $13.5B [Actual], up 10.3% year over year. Diluted EPS was $4.10 [Actual], up 12%. Operating earnings were about $1.4B [Actual/Estimate], with a 10.5% operating margin [Actual/Estimate]. All four segments grew revenue, including Aerospace at $3.28B, Marine Systems at $4.34B, Combat Systems at $2.28B, and Technologies at roughly $3.55B [Actual/Estimate from filing summaries].

Backlog is the core asset. Total estimated contract value was $188.4B at quarter-end [Actual], including $130.8B of backlog [Actual] and $57.6B of estimated potential contract value from unfunded IDIQ contracts and unexercised options [Actual]. That provides multi-year visibility, especially in submarine and defense programs.

The valuation is the restraint. GD closed at $373.54 [Actual], a new 52-week high. Market cap sources range around $98B-$101B [Actual/Estimate]. Full-year EPS guidance was reportedly raised to about $16.45-$16.55 [Actual from WSJ/company result reporting]. That implies roughly 22.6x forward EPS [Estimate]. For a high-quality defense contractor, this is not extreme, but it is no longer cheap.

RS2 conclusion: GD is a Hold / buy-on-pullback. The company is high quality and strategically important, but new money should wait for a better entry below $335-$345 or for clearer evidence that Marine Systems execution and Gulfstream deliveries can sustain upside without margin disappointment.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Defense demand is structurally favorable. U.S. and allied defense budgets are supported by great-power competition, naval modernization, munitions replenishment, armored vehicles, cybersecurity, and geopolitical tensions. GD's Marine Systems and Combat Systems segments are well aligned with these priorities.

Submarine demand is particularly strategic. Nuclear submarine production, maintenance, and modernization are critical to U.S. deterrence and AUKUS-related capacity. This supports backlog but also creates execution pressure in skilled labor, shipyard capacity, suppliers, and cost control.

Business aviation is more cyclical. Gulfstream benefits from wealthy individuals, corporations, and international customers, but demand can slow with macro uncertainty, financing conditions, and geopolitical events. The WSJ reported that Middle East conflict affected aerospace labor availability/order timing context in Q1 commentary [Actual/Reported].

Defense IT/Technologies demand is steady but competitive. Government services and mission systems benefit from modernization but face recompete and margin pressure.

Macro score: favorable defense budget environment, positive backlog visibility, mixed aerospace cycle.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Base rate for defense primes: large defense contractors can compound through backlog, cost-plus/fixed-price program execution, dividends, buybacks, and budget durability. Upside is usually steady rather than explosive.

Base rate for shipbuilding programs: backlog visibility is high, but schedule delays, labor shortages, supplier issues, and cost overruns are common. Investors should not treat backlog as risk-free revenue.

Base rate for business jets: premium products can be highly profitable, but deliveries and book-to-bill are cyclical. Gulfstream margin recovery is a key swing factor.

Base rate for defense stocks at highs: defense quality often trades at premium multiples during geopolitical tension. Buying at peak sentiment can reduce forward returns.

Base-rate conclusion: GD is a strong compounder, but price discipline matters because the market already recognizes defense visibility.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

GD's quality comes from strategic assets that are difficult to replicate. Nuclear submarine shipbuilding, Gulfstream jets, combat vehicles, munitions, secure communications, and defense IT all have high barriers to entry.

Marine Systems is the visibility anchor. Q1 2026 Marine Systems revenue was about $4.34B [Actual/Estimate], and segment growth was strong. Programs such as Virginia-class and Columbia-class submarines create long-duration demand.

Aerospace is the margin/cycle lever. Gulfstream business jets are premium assets, and improved deliveries/mix can lift margins. However, aerospace orders and labor availability can vary.

Combat Systems benefits from global rearmament. Armored vehicles, artillery, munitions, and land systems are strategically relevant in the current defense environment.

Technologies provides diversified services and mission systems. It adds recurring/recompete revenue and exposure to defense IT modernization.

Business quality score: 13/15. Barriers, backlog, and strategic relevance are high. Deductions: shipbuilding execution risk and business-jet cyclicality.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Backlog has second-order margin risk. Large submarine and shipbuilding programs bring visibility, but labor/supplier bottlenecks can reduce profitability if execution slips.

Defense budget growth has second-order working-capital effects. Faster production ramps can require inventory, suppliers, hiring, and capex before cash collection.

Gulfstream delivery cadence has second-order earnings leverage. Higher deliveries and mix can lift Aerospace margins and EPS faster than revenue.

Geopolitical tension has second-order multiple effects. It can raise defense multiples, but also create buy points if budget politics or program costs disappoint.
