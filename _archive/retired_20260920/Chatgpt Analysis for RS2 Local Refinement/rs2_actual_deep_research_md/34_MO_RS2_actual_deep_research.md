## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Altria is a domestic U.S. nicotine cash-flow company. Its core economic engine remains Marlboro and the smokeable products segment, supported by pricing power, high margins, dividends, and repurchases. Its strategic problem is that cigarette volumes decline structurally, while the company's reduced-risk portfolio has not yet proven it can replace the economics of combustible tobacco at scale.

Q1 2026 looked financially strong. Net revenues were $5.428B [Actual], up 3.2%; revenues net of excise taxes were $4.758B [Actual], up 5.3%; reported diluted EPS was $1.30 [Actual]; and adjusted diluted EPS was $1.32 [Actual], up 7.3%. Management reaffirmed 2026 adjusted diluted EPS guidance of $5.56-$5.72 [Actual], representing 2.5%-5.5% growth from a 2025 base of $5.42 [Actual].

The issue is quality of growth. Smokeable revenues net of excise taxes rose 5.2% in Q1 2026 [Actual], while domestic cigarette shipment volume fell 2.4% reported and about 4% adjusted for trade inventory movements [Actual]. Total domestic cigarette industry volume fell an estimated 5% adjusted for trade inventory movements [Actual]. Altria is still able to price through volume decline, but the volume slope remains negative.

The oral segment is not yet a clear rescue. Q1 2026 oral tobacco revenue net of excise taxes rose 2.9% [Actual], but domestic shipment volume fell 3.1% reported and about 8.5% adjusted for trade inventory movements [Actual]. on! shipment volume rose 17.6% [Actual], but on!'s share of the nicotine pouch category fell 4.2 percentage points year over year to 13.4% [Actual]. That is a mixed signal: category exposure is positive, competitive position is not.

At about $71-$72 per share and 2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $5.64 [Estimate], MO trades around 12.7x forward adjusted EPS [Estimate]. The annualized dividend of $4.24 per share [Actual/Estimate] implies about a 5.9% yield [Actual/Estimate], with a payout ratio around 75% of guided adjusted EPS [Estimate]. This is reasonable but not screaming cheap given regulatory, volume, litigation, and reduced-risk execution risk.

RS2 conclusion: MO is a Hold for income investors and a selective Buy only on pullbacks. The dividend is attractive and likely supportable, but upside is capped unless Altria proves better reduced-risk traction or the stock trades at a materially higher yield.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

The U.S. tobacco macro is structurally declining in combustibles. Adult nicotine consumers continue shifting away from cigarettes toward oral nicotine pouches, e-vapor, heated tobacco, and illicit disposable products. That creates a long-term volume headwind for Altria's core smokeable franchise.

Consumer stress is relevant. Altria said cigarette industry decline was affected by continued discretionary income pressures on adult nicotine consumers [Actual]. The Q1 2026 discount cigarette retail share rose 2.4 percentage points year over year to 33.3% [Actual]. That implies pressure on premium affordability and downtrading risk.

Regulation is the dominant macro variable. FDA menthol/flavor policy, nicotine standards, e-vapor authorization, illicit-vapor enforcement, and state-level rules can alter category economics. The FDA has authorized certain NJOY products, including menthol e-vapor products, but the regulatory path remains uneven [Actual]. Altria also assumes NJOY ACE does not return to the market in 2026 [Actual].

The illicit e-vapor market remains a major competitive distortion. Unauthorized disposable products can reduce cigarette consumption while also limiting legal e-vapor profitability. Government enforcement has increased, including multi-agency seizures of illicit vaping products, but the market remains difficult to police [Actual].

Macro score: negative for combustible volume, mixed for reduced-risk alternatives, positive for yield demand if rates fall.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Base rate for tobacco incumbents: mature tobacco companies can produce exceptional shareholder returns through pricing power, high margins, low capex, dividends, and buybacks even while volumes decline. Altria has historically fit this pattern.

Base rate for combustible decline: pricing can offset volume for many years, but not indefinitely. The critical variable is whether volume decline stays manageable, roughly mid-single-digit, or accelerates into high-single-digit/low-double-digit territory.

Base rate for reduced-risk transitions: incumbents often struggle when new categories cannibalize high-margin legacy products and require different innovation/regulatory capabilities. Altria's JUUL history, NJOY impairments, and on! share pressure show this risk is not theoretical.

Base rate for high-yield equities: a high dividend yield can be attractive, but it can also signal low growth, litigation/regulatory risk, and limited reinvestment opportunities. Dividend coverage must be modeled from future cash flows, not historical reputation.

Base-rate conclusion: MO can work as an income/security-return story, but it should be valued as a declining-core cash machine with uncertain transformation, not as a growth compounder.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Altria's core quality remains the smokeable products segment. Q1 2026 smokeable net revenues were $4.758B [Actual], revenues net of excise taxes were $4.110B [Actual], reported OCI was $2.673B [Actual], and adjusted OCI was $2.676B [Actual], up 6.3%. Adjusted OCI margin was approximately 65.1% [Actual/Estimate]. This is an elite margin profile.

Marlboro remains a powerful brand. Marlboro's total cigarette retail share was 39.7% [Actual], down 1.4 points year over year, while its premium-segment share was 59.5% [Actual], up 0.1 point year over year and 0.2 points sequentially. That means Marlboro's premium positioning remains resilient, but total-category share pressure and discount growth matter.

The oral tobacco segment is high-margin but competitively stressed. Q1 oral net revenues were $669M [Actual], revenues net of excise taxes were $647M [Actual], and adjusted OCI was $436M [Actual]. Adjusted OCI margin was 67.4% [Actual]. However, total oral retail share fell 5.5 percentage points year over year to 29.0% [Actual], and on!'s nicotine-pouch share fell to 13.4% [Actual].

The e-vapor/reduced-risk platform is not yet a solved problem. NJOY gives Altria FDA-authorized products, but NJOY ACE is assumed not to return to market in 2026 [Actual], and prior e-vapor impairment charges damaged confidence. Horizon/IQOS-related heated tobacco optionality exists, but commercialization economics remain uncertain [Assumption].

Capital return is strong. Altria paid $1.8B of dividends in Q1 2026 [Actual] and repurchased 4.5M shares for $280M at an average price of $62.33 [Actual]. It had $720M remaining on a $2B repurchase authorization expiring December 31, 2026 [Actual].

Business quality score: 9.5/15. The cash-flow engine is excellent; the growth/transformation engine is unproven.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Pricing power can create a hidden risk. First-order effect: higher prices offset lower cigarette volume. Second-order effect: higher prices push stressed adult smokers into discount, illicit, e-vapor, or oral alternatives, weakening Marlboro's long-term consumer base.

Illicit e-vapor competition has two-way effects. It accelerates combustible volume decline while also making legal reduced-risk products less profitable. Better enforcement would help NJOY and legal category participants, but enforcement is uncertain.

Repurchases are accretive only if the terminal cash flow is durable. Buying stock at $62.33 in Q1 [Actual] looks attractive, but buybacks above fair value would reduce flexibility.

Dividend growth is psychologically important. Altria's investor base values predictable income. A dividend slowdown would damage the multiple even if the payout remains covered.

Reduced-risk failure has compounding cost. Every year without a scalable non-combustible profit pool increases dependence on a shrinking cigarette base.

## SECTION 4. Scenarios

Bull case:

Smokeable volume declines stay around 4%-5%, pricing offsets volume, on! PLUS improves share, NJOY/regulatory enforcement stabilizes e-vapor, and EPS grows 4%-6% annually. Dividend grows mid-single digits and the stock trades at 13x-14x EPS. Fair value: $78-$85 [Assumption], plus dividend income.

Base case:

Smokeable volume declines mid-single digits, pricing offsets most volume pressure, oral remains mixed, NJOY contributes little in 2026, and adjusted EPS grows 2.5%-4%. Stock trades around 11x-13x EPS and total return is mostly dividend-driven. Fair value: $64-$75 [Assumption].

Bear case:

Cigarette volume decline accelerates, discount share rises, Marlboro loses premium share, on! keeps losing nicotine-pouch share, e-vapor remains impaired, and regulatory/litigation costs rise. EPS growth stalls and the market demands a 7%+ yield. Fair value: $50-$60 [Assumption].

Stress case:

A menthol/nicotine standard or major regulatory action combines with faster combustible downtrading and failed reduced-risk execution. Dividend growth halts or payout credibility weakens. Stock could fall below $50 [Assumption].

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

The market may underappreciate Altria's ability to extract cash from a declining cigarette market for longer than expected. That has historically been the tobacco investor's edge.

The market may also be correctly skeptical that reduced-risk products will replace cigarette economics. on! share loss, NJOY ACE absence, and prior impairments show that management execution in new categories is not yet proven.

Horizon conclusion: the long-term edge exists only if the investor buys at a sufficient yield and accepts limited growth. At current price, the edge is moderate, not strong.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

Bullish trap: seeing a 5.9% yield and assuming safety. A high yield is only valuable if the earnings base remains durable.

Bearish trap: assuming declining cigarette volumes mean immediate dividend impairment. Tobacco pricing power can offset volume for years.

Narrative trap: over-believing "smoke-free future" language before reduced-risk products show profit replacement.

Anchoring trap: comparing MO to its historical dividend-growth reputation without adjusting for current nicotine-category competition.

Behavioral rule: do not buy for yield alone. Demand evidence of manageable volume decline and reduced-risk progress.
