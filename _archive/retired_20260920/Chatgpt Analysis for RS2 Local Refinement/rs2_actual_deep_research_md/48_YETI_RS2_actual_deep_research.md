## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

YETI is a premium outdoor and drinkware brand with strong consumer recognition, but its RS2 profile is mixed. Q1 2026 sales increased 8%, with wholesale sales up 19%, U.S. sales up 8%, international sales up 9%, Coolers & Equipment up 11%, and Drinkware up 5% [Actual]. The issue is profitability: EPS decreased 35% to $0.13 and adjusted EPS decreased 16% to $0.26, including a $0.09 unfavorable net tariff impact [Actual].

At $49.69, the stock trades at about 25.4x trailing EPS of $1.96 [Actual]. That is a full multiple for a consumer-products company still dealing with tariff pressure and margin inconsistency. The brand is valuable, but the current setup requires confidence that revenue growth can translate back into EPS growth.

Core view: Hold. YETI is not broken, but it is not cheap enough to ignore margin and tariff risk. Buy interest improves below $42 or after a clearer evidence that margins are normalizing.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

YETI sells premium discretionary goods. Consumer income, outdoor participation, gift demand, retail inventory discipline, and tariffs all matter. The company benefits from a resilient affluent customer base, but coolers, bags, and drinkware are not essential purchases.

Tariffs are a direct macro/policy risk. Q1 adjusted EPS included a $0.09 unfavorable net impact from incremental tariffs [Actual], which is significant relative to $0.26 adjusted EPS. If tariff cost pressure persists, YETI either absorbs the hit, raises prices, or risks demand elasticity.

Macro conclusion: revenue demand is holding, but margins remain exposed to policy and consumer trade-down risk.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

Premium consumer brands can be excellent long-term investments when they combine pricing power, category expansion, and global distribution. The base-rate problem is that many once-hot consumer brands mature into slower-growth lifestyle brands with volatile margins and inventory cycles.

YETI's Q1 sales growth is encouraging, especially wholesale recovery and international growth [Actual]. However, adjusted EPS declined despite revenue growth [Actual]. That is the base-rate warning: brand strength does not guarantee operating leverage in a tariff-heavy or promotional consumer environment.

Base-rate score: neutral. Brand quality is real, but the financial pattern is not yet high-conviction.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

YETI's core strength is brand equity. It has moved from coolers into drinkware, bags, and broader outdoor lifestyle categories. International sales growth of 9% in Q1 2026 suggests room outside the U.S. [Actual]. The direct-to-consumer channel, product innovation, and premium positioning can support healthy gross margins over a cycle.

Weaknesses include category saturation, competition, counterfeit or lower-priced substitutes, and reliance on discretionary purchases. Drinkware growth of 5% is solid but not explosive [Actual], and the brand must keep generating newness to avoid becoming a mature replacement-cycle business.

Quality score: 7/10. Strong brand, but consumer cyclicality and margin pressure keep it below elite status.

## SECTION 3.5. Second-Order Effects

Tariffs can create delayed second-order effects. If YETI raises prices to protect margins, near-term revenue may look fine while volume elasticity appears later. If it absorbs tariff cost, brand demand remains healthy but earnings power is lower.

Wholesale recovery can also have second-order effects. Strong wholesale growth improves distribution, but it can shift mix away from direct-to-consumer economics if not balanced carefully.

## SECTION 4. Scenarios

Bear case: tariff pressure persists, consumer demand slows, and EPS remains under pressure. Fair value falls into the $30s [Estimate].

Base case: sales grow mid-to-high single digits, tariffs are managed, and EPS recovers toward $2.50. Fair value is around $45 to $52 [Estimate].

Bull case: international growth accelerates, new categories scale, and margins normalize. Fair value can reach the low-to-mid $60s [Estimate].

Probability-weighted view: balanced. Current price does not provide much margin for error.

Process rule: do not upgrade unless revenue growth turns into EPS growth.
