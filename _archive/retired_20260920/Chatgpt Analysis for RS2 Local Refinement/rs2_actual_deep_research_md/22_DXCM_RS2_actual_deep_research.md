## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

Dexcom is one of the global leaders in continuous glucose monitoring ("CGM") and broader glucose biosensing. Its core products help people with diabetes track glucose in real time, and its growth runway is expanding from intensive insulin users toward broader type 2 diabetes, over-the-counter wellness/metabolic use cases, pregnancy/gestational diabetes, inpatient use, and automated insulin delivery ecosystems.

The current setup is attractive. Q1 2026 revenue grew 15% reported and 12% organic to $1.192 billion, U.S. revenue grew 11%, international revenue grew 26% reported and 17% organic, GAAP operating margin expanded to 21.4%, and non-GAAP operating margin expanded to 22.2%. Dexcom reiterated FY2026 revenue guidance of $5.16 billion to $5.25 billion, or 11% to 13% growth, and raised non-GAAP operating margin guidance to 23% to 23.5% and adjusted EBITDA margin guidance to 31% to 31.5%. Management also said it expects over $1 billion of free cash flow in 2026.

At $71.25, DXCM trades at roughly 5.4x FY2026 guided revenue, about 16.9x to 17.5x FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA, and around 28x to 31x a rough FY2026 earnings-power estimate depending on tax, share count, and non-GAAP adjustments. This is not a deep-value multiple, but it is reasonable for a category leader with double-digit revenue growth, expanding margins, strong free cash flow, and a large cash balance.

Final view: DXCM is a Buy / staged accumulate. The stock is attractive if the investor can tolerate CGM competition and reimbursement/pricing risk. The best execution is to start a position now and add on pullbacks below $65 or after Q2 confirms that Q1 margin gains were not a one-quarter spike.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

Dexcom benefits from strong secular demand:

- Diabetes prevalence remains high and rising globally.
- CGM penetration is still expanding beyond type 1 diabetes into type 2 diabetes.
- Payers increasingly recognize CGM's clinical and economic value.
- Automated insulin delivery and smart dosing systems require reliable CGM data.
- Consumers are increasingly interested in metabolic health and glucose feedback.
- International reimbursement expansion can drive multi-year growth.

The macro backdrop also contains pressure points:

- Healthcare budgets and payer utilization management can slow adoption.
- CGM competition can create pricing pressure.
- Consumer discretionary pressure can affect out-of-pocket Stelo/wellness demand.
- Manufacturing scale and sensor reliability are critical.
- Regulatory scrutiny rises as CGMs move into broader, lower-acuity populations.

Dexcom's Q1 2026 showed that the macro tailwinds are still active. International revenue grew 26% reported, and Q1 strategic highlights included expanded U.S. launch of Dexcom G7 15 Day, enhanced Smart Meal Logging features for Stelo, and clinical evidence for Dexcom G7 in type 2 diabetes patients not using insulin.

Macro conclusion: favorable. CGM remains a secular growth category, not a mature device market.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for medical device leaders with recurring consumable revenue is strong. Durable device consumables can earn premium multiples when they combine clinical value, reimbursement, brand trust, high gross margins, and manufacturing scale. CGM has those traits, but competition and payer pressure can reduce the premium.

Dexcom's recent base rate:

- FY2025 revenue: $4.662 billion, up about 16%.
- FY2025 GAAP operating income: $911.8 million.
- FY2025 GAAP operating margin: 19.6%.
- FY2025 non-GAAP operating income: $969.3 million.
- FY2025 non-GAAP operating margin: 20.8%.
- Q1 2026 revenue growth: 15% reported, 12% organic.
- FY2026 revenue guidance: 11% to 13% growth.
- FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margin guidance: 31% to 31.5%.

This is a strong profile. Dexcom is no longer a hypergrowth company, but it is a scaled growth medical device company with expanding margins and large free cash flow.

Base-rate positives:

- Recurring sensor revenue.
- Large underpenetrated markets.
- Strong brand and clinical evidence.
- Expanding international access.
- High cash balance.
- Manufacturing scale benefits.
- OTC Stelo and broader biosensing optionality.

Base-rate negatives:

- Abbott FreeStyle Libre is a formidable global competitor.
- Medtronic and Abbott partnership can improve pump-integrated alternatives.
- Payer pricing pressure can emerge as CGM becomes mainstream.
- Category growth can decelerate as insulin-intensive markets mature.
- Device recalls or accuracy concerns can damage trust.

Base-rate conclusion: DXCM deserves a premium multiple, but it must keep showing growth beyond the core insulin-intensive market.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

Dexcom's business quality is high because CGM sensors are recurring, clinically important, data-rich, and integrated into diabetes-management ecosystems. Unlike many medical devices that are one-time implants or capital equipment, CGM produces repeat revenue as sensors are replaced and users remain on therapy.

Quality strengths:

- Category leadership in CGM.
- Strong brand with patients, clinicians, and ecosystem partners.
- Recurring consumable revenue model.
- Large cash balance and undrawn revolver.
- Strong gross margins.
- Operating margin expansion in Q1 2026.
- International growth above U.S. growth.
- G7 15 Day improves wear duration and economics.
- Stelo expands OTC and non-insulin use-case optionality.
- Clinical evidence supports broader type 2 diabetes use.

Quality weaknesses:

- Competition is intense.
- Product reliability and accuracy are mission-critical.
- Reimbursement and coverage are central to adoption.
- Manufacturing scale-up must be precise.
- The OTC/wellness market may have lower retention or lower pricing.
- Convertible notes can complicate diluted share count and EPS.

Business quality score: very high. Dexcom is a scaled growth medical device franchise with software-like recurring traits, but it is not protected from price and share competition.

- Clinical evidence in non-insulin type 2 diabetes can support reimbursement expansion.

Second-order conclusion: the opportunity is not just more insulin users; the upside depends on Dexcom converting CGM into a broader biosensing platform without losing premium economics.

Bull scenario:

Abbott and Medtronic pressure pricing, Stelo adoption disappoints, payers tighten coverage, and revenue growth slows below high single digits. Margins flatten or compress, and the stock derates to the low-to-mid 20s P/E. Stock value: $40 to $55.

Stress scenario:

A major product-quality, accuracy, cybersecurity, or reimbursement event hits CGM confidence. In that case Dexcom remains solvent and cash-rich, but the premium medical device multiple can compress sharply.

Scenario conclusion: favorable skew. The base case supports the current price, and the bull case offers meaningful upside.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

Short-term investors may still anchor on the 2024/2025 period when CGM growth expectations reset and the market worried about U.S. sales force execution and competition. The longer-horizon view is that Dexcom is now a scaled, profitable CGM leader with improving margins and multiple future-market options.

What the market may miss:

- Q1 2026 margin expansion was substantial.
- International growth remains strong.
- G7 15 Day and Stelo broaden the product ladder.
- Non-insulin type 2 diabetes evidence could expand the reimbursable market.
- Dexcom's cash balance gives strategic flexibility.

What the market may overestimate:

- That CGM will remain a premium-priced duopoly forever.
- That OTC CGM users will retain like insulin-intensive users.
- That every broader-market user creates equal lifetime value.

Horizon conclusion: DXCM is best judged over 2 to 4 years. The market may be underpricing the durability of CGM adoption and margin recovery.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

The bearish behavioral trap is anchoring to prior Dexcom execution disappointments and assuming competition has permanently broken the model. The bullish trap is assuming CGM growth is so obvious that price and reimbursement never matter.

Biases to monitor:

- Recency bias: over-weighting past CGM execution stumbles.
- TAM intoxication: assuming every prediabetes or wellness user is economically equivalent to a reimbursed diabetes user.
- Duopoly complacency: underestimating Abbott and Medtronic.
- Margin extrapolation: treating Q1 operating margin expansion as automatic.
- Product-love bias: confusing patient enthusiasm with payer economics.

Behavioral rule: buy in stages and demand evidence that revenue growth and margin expansion are both durable.

Dexcom's Stelo and broader biosensing markets may have lower reimbursement, lower retention, and lower margins than core diabetes users. At over 5x revenue and around 17x EBITDA, the stock is not cheap if growth slows below 10%.

The strongest bull rebuttal:

Dexcom just delivered 15% reported growth, 12% organic growth, strong international momentum, and large margin expansion. FY2026 guidance implies $5.2 billion of revenue, 31%+ adjusted EBITDA margins, and over $1 billion of FCF. The company has $2.42 billion of cash and marketable securities. CGM adoption is still underpenetrated globally, and Stelo/G7 15 Day provide incremental growth options.

My synthesis:

The bear case is real but already partly priced in. Dexcom remains a category leader with improving profitability and reasonable valuation. Buy in stages.
