## SECTION 0. Analysis Overview

HealthEquity is the largest independent health savings account ("HSA") custodian by account volume and a major consumer-directed benefits ("CDB") administrator. Its model combines HSA account administration, custodial revenue on HSA cash, interchange revenue, complementary benefit accounts, member engagement, and increasingly investment balances. The company is a healthcare financial platform rather than a conventional healthcare services provider.

The current setup is attractive. For fiscal 2026, revenue rose 9% to $1.31 billion, net income rose 123% to $215.2 million, non-GAAP EPS rose 28% to $4.00, adjusted EBITDA rose 20% to $566.0 million, and total HSA assets grew 14% to $36.5 billion. Q1 fiscal 2027 continued the pattern: revenue rose 7% to $354.6 million, net income rose 29% to $69.4 million, adjusted EBITDA rose 17% to $164.5 million, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 46%, non-GAAP EPS rose 28% to $1.24, and total HSA assets grew 19% to $37.1 billion. Management raised FY2027 guidance to revenue of $1.410 billion to $1.420 billion, adjusted EBITDA of $625 million to $633 million, GAAP EPS of $2.88 to $2.95, and non-GAAP EPS of $4.66 to $4.73.

At $95.31, HQY trades at roughly 20.3x FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance, about 13.3x EV/FY2027 adjusted EBITDA, and around 5.4x FY2027 revenue. That is a reasonable valuation for a market leader with 40%+ EBITDA margins, HSA asset growth, buyback support, and a rate/cash-repricing tailwind. It is not dirt cheap on GAAP earnings, but the free-cash-flow and EBITDA profile is strong.

Final view: HQY is a Buy / staged accumulate. The position should be sized with respect for interest-rate sensitivity, HSA policy risk, cybersecurity/fraud risk, and leverage, but the business quality and valuation justify ownership.

## SECTION 1. Macro Context

HealthEquity benefits from several macro and structural trends:

- U.S. healthcare costs continue shifting more responsibility to consumers.
- High-deductible health plans and HSAs remain a core employer-benefit architecture.
- Consumers need tax-advantaged ways to save, spend, and invest for healthcare.
- Employers and benefits advisors need integrated administration for HSAs, FSAs, HRAs, COBRA, commuter benefits, and related CDB accounts.
- HSA account balances can compound over time as members mature and invest.
- Higher interest rates and contractual cash repricing can lift custodial revenue.

The macro backdrop is especially important because HealthEquity earns custodial revenue from HSA cash. Q1 FY2027 service revenue grew 3%, custodial revenue grew 11%, and interchange revenue grew 5%. The company disclosed $17.5 billion of HSA cash and a maturity schedule showing $16.5 billion of HSA cash with an average annualized yield of 3.6%, excluding $1.0 billion of floating-rate contracts. That means the business has a meaningful rate component, not just account-administration volume.

Macro risks:

- Lower short rates can reduce custodial revenue over time.
- HSA tax treatment or high-deductible health plan rules could change.
- Employment, wage, and benefits enrollment trends affect account growth.
- Equity-market declines can reduce HSA investment balances.
- Political scrutiny of high-deductible plans can affect the HSA ecosystem.

Macro conclusion: favorable but rate-sensitive. The business benefits from consumer-directed healthcare and account maturation, but custodial yield is a real variable.

## SECTION 1.5. Base Rate Check

The base rate for benefits administrators is usually moderate growth, operational complexity, and margin pressure. HealthEquity is better than that base rate because it owns a scaled HSA custody platform with balances that can compound and generate custodial revenue.

Relevant base-rate facts:

- HealthEquity serves more than 17 million accounts.
- It is the No. 1 HSA provider and says 1 in 4 U.S. HSAs are with the company.
- Q1 FY2027 HSAs were 10.6 million, up 8% year over year.
- HSAs with investments were 909,000, up 18%.
- HSA investments were $19.6 billion, up 38%.
- Total HSA assets were $37.1 billion, up 19%.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 46% in Q1 FY2027.

This is a better-than-average financial platform base rate. The scale, member balances, and account longevity make HealthEquity more interesting than a typical administrative processor.

Base-rate cautions:

- The business is tied to U.S. tax and healthcare rules.
- Custodial revenue can be rate-cycle sensitive.
- Acquisitions and integrations have historically mattered.
- Cybersecurity and fraudulent account activity are high-impact risks.
- Debt and buybacks must be managed carefully.

Base-rate conclusion: above-average quality and margin profile, with policy/rate sensitivity as the main offsets.

## SECTION 2. Business Quality

HealthEquity's moat comes from scale, distribution, account relationships, employer/advisor/health-plan partnerships, custodial economics, technology, and member engagement. Once an employer or plan is integrated into an HSA/CDB provider, switching can be disruptive, especially when accounts, cards, claims, tax reporting, investments, and benefits administration are embedded.

Business strengths:

- Largest independent HSA custodian by account volume.
- More than 100,000 clients and 200+ network partners.
- HSA assets of $37.1 billion.
- High adjusted EBITDA margin.
- Mix of service, custodial, and interchange revenue.
- Strong operating cash flow.
- Buyback capacity and demonstrated repurchases.
- HSA investment balances growing faster than total assets.
- Technology and mobile engagement improving.
- Account maturity can increase lifetime value over time.

Business weaknesses:

- Rate sensitivity through custodial yield.
- Debt from past acquisition activity.
- Heavy dependence on HSA tax advantages and high-deductible plans.
- Cybersecurity and fraudulent-account exposure.
- Competition from banks, benefit administrators, payroll vendors, insurers, retirement platforms, and fintechs.
- Some revenue growth depends on macro rates rather than pure account growth.

Business quality score: high. HealthEquity is a scaled, high-margin healthcare financial platform, not a low-margin benefits processor.

## SECTION 4.5. Horizon Arbitrage

Short-term investors may view HQY as a rate-beneficiary stock and worry about peak custodial yield. The longer-horizon opportunity is that HSA accounts and investment balances can compound over many years, and member engagement can increase lifetime value even if rates normalize.

What the market may miss:

- HSA investments grew 38% year over year.
- HSAs with investments grew 18%.
- Account maturity can increase per-account economics.
- Q1 adjusted EBITDA margin of 46% shows operating leverage.
- The $1.0 billion incremental buyback authorization is large relative to market cap.

What the market may overprice:

- Current custodial yield as a permanent run rate.
- HSA asset growth without considering market drawdowns.
- Repurchases as automatically value-creating despite leverage.

Horizon conclusion: the best underwriting horizon is 2 to 4 years, focused on account growth, asset growth, and margin durability through rate cycles.

## SECTION 5.5. Behavioral

The bearish behavioral trap is assuming all of HealthEquity's earnings are merely a rate-cycle windfall and ignoring account/asset compounding. The bullish trap is assuming HSA growth and current yields are permanent and policy-proof.

Biases to monitor:

- Rate-anchor bias: over-weighting current custodial yield.
- Policy complacency: assuming HSA tax advantages cannot change.
- EBITDA fixation: ignoring GAAP EPS and SBC.
- Buyback optimism: assuming all repurchases are accretive despite debt.
- Healthcare-affordability narrative bias: assuming consumer-directed care always expands politically.

Behavioral rule: buy because the platform and valuation are good, but revisit if rates, HSA policy, or cybersecurity/fraud risk changes materially.

The business also depends on HSA tax advantages and high-deductible health plans, both of which can become political targets. The company carries nearly $1 billion of debt and uses meaningful non-GAAP adjustments.

The strongest bull rebuttal:

HealthEquity is not just a rate trade. Q1 FY2027 showed 17% adjusted EBITDA growth and 46% adjusted EBITDA margin. The company raised guidance and authorized $1 billion of additional buybacks.

My synthesis:

The bear case is real but manageable. HQY is a good platform at a fair price, with upside from asset growth, operating leverage, and buybacks.
